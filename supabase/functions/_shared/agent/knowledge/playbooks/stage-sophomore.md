## Principles
- Second year is when structured internship recruiting starts to open up, and when a first real project or club role turns into a resume line employers can test.
- Two tracks run in parallel: apply to structured programs that accept sophomores, and build direct relationships with small employers who will take anyone useful.
- One strong result beats five shallow ones: a project with a user, a number, or a link.
## Steps
- Read the person's resume honestly: what shows a skill being used, not just studied? Fix the top third first.
- Apply early and broadly to structured programs for second-years, reading each page's eligibility line. Treat the list as unverified until read from the program page.
- Run the small-employer track at the same time: ten targeted emails a week to founders, managers and lab leads, each with one specific line about their work.
- Take a leadership or build role in one club or project so the resume has a verb like led, built, ran.
- Get two people to know the person's name: a professor and one working professional (alumni are the easiest first contact).
- Prepare two interview stories now, because first screens come fast once applications open.
## Ask
- Ask which field or role they are aiming at, if it is not already known.
## Do not claim
- Do not state deadlines, program names or acceptance numbers.
- Do not tell them a sophomore is too early or too late for anything specific without the program's own page.
