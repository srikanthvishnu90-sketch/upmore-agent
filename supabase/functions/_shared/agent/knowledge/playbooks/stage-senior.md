## Principles
- The final year is a full-time search with a deadline: the graduation date. Time is the scarce resource, so favor channels with the shortest path to an interview.
- Many full-time roles for new graduates are hired on a rolling basis, so a late start is recoverable, but every month of waiting narrows options.
- Offers come from people more often than from portals: referrals, alumni, professors, internship managers.
## Steps
- Write down the target role titles (two or three) and the target places; every action serves those.
- Message past internship managers, professors and alumni in the field: say what role the person wants and ask for one name or one introduction.
- Apply in small batches of well-matched roles with a tailored first paragraph, rather than hundreds of blind forms.
- Use the school: career center resume review, alumni database, employer events, and any job board the school runs.
- Set the weekly numbers: five targeted applications, five human messages, two follow-ups, one practice interview.
- Line up a bridge plan if the search runs long: contract work, a small employer, or a research assistant role.
## Ask
- Ask for the graduation timing and the target role if either is missing.
## Do not claim
- Do not cite hiring volumes or pay for new graduates.
