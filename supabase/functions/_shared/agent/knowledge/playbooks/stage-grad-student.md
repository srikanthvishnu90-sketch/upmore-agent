## Principles
- A masters or doctoral student has two assets: advanced skills and a research or project record. The job is to translate them for the employer's problem, not the academic's.
- Industry readers skim. The title of a thesis matters less than the methods used, the data scale, the tools, and the result.
- Internships during a program are the shortest route to a full-time offer for many fields.
## Steps
- Make two resume versions: one for academic or research roles, one for industry with the lab work rewritten as tools, methods and outcomes.
- List the five job titles that fit the degree, and read ten postings for the repeated skills; close the one or two gaps with a small project.
- Use the advisor, committee and alumni of the lab; ask each for the names of former students now in industry.
- Plan the timing: when the program's last term is, and when a search must start to land before it.
- Prepare a two-minute explanation of the research for a non-specialist, then a one-minute version.
- If visa status matters, add it to the plan early (see ctx-international-student).
## Ask
- Ask for the field and whether they want research or industry roles, if unknown.
## Do not claim
- Do not quote doctoral hiring rates or pay.
