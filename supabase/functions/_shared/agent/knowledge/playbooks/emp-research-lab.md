## Principles
- Research positions with a professor are one of the most reachable early experiences, because labs need help and professors say yes to students who show specific interest and reliability.
- A specific email about the professor's actual papers beats a generic request. Show that the person read something.
- Funded summer research programs run at many universities and are for students from other schools too; each has its own rules and dates on its own page.
## Steps
- Make a list of professors in the department whose work interests the person: read the lab page and the titles of recent papers.
- Write each a short email: who the person is, one specific thing in the lab's work, relevant courses or skills, and a request to help in some small way for credit, pay or volunteer time.
- Attach a resume and a transcript if asked.
- Go to office hours of a course professor, and ask how first-years or sophomores can get involved.
- Search for undergraduate research programs and the school's research office listings; read the eligibility and timeline of each.
- Treat the first term as a trial: show up, finish small tasks, then ask for a bigger one.
## Ask
- Ask for the field or department.
## Do not claim
- Do not state program names, deadlines, or stipends.
- Do not say a professor will respond or that a position exists.
