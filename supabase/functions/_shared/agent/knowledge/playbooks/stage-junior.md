## Principles
- The summer before the last year is the internship that most often becomes the full-time offer, so this stage is the highest-stakes one. Treat the search as a project with a start date now.
- Employers want evidence that the person can do the job: past internship, project, research, or leadership with outcomes.
- If prior experience is thin, a strong project plus targeted outreach still competes, especially at mid-size and small employers.
## Steps
- Work backward from the application windows the person finds on each target's own site; list them in a tracker with the date found.
- Rewrite the resume around outcomes: what changed because of the person's work, with real numbers only if they are true.
- Build the pipeline in three tiers: reach employers that recruit on a fixed calendar, mid-size employers that hire as needed, and small firms reached by direct email.
- Ask for referrals from anyone who has worked at a target; a referral is a short, specific, easy-to-forward note (see how-referral-ask).
- Start interview preparation before the first invite: two stories, one technical or case drill a week for their field.
- Keep a fallback: if offers do not come by the spring, move to the small-employer track and research roles, which keep hiring later.
## Ask
- Ask whether they have any internship or project experience yet, if not stated.
## Do not claim
- Do not quote conversion rates, pay, or deadlines.
- Do not promise a return offer.
