## Format facts (sourced)

- **REPE process (Wall Street Prep):** typically three rounds — an informational first round (résumé, interest in the firm, long-term goals), a technical second round (valuation, property types, market views), and an in-person superday with senior and mid-level interviewers that is likely to conclude with a modeling test and possibly a case study. Expectations differ by entry point: undergraduates are pushed less on technicals and more on hunger and interest; former bankers face the highest modeling bar; brokerage / asset-management candidates face higher real-estate-knowledge expectations. Source: Wall Street Prep, Real Estate Private Equity career guide — https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/real-estate-private-equity-career-guide/
- **REPE process (Leland, second attestation, durations differ in detail):** phone screens, then a technical interview, then a case study (timed or take-home), then a half- to full-day superday. Treat the exact minutes / hours as firm-specific, not universal. Source: Leland, Real Estate Private Equity Interview Guide — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview
- **Technical interview (A.CRE):** after the HR screen, a multi-hour technical interview built from three parts — a case study / skills test, a presentation of your conclusions, and one-on-one interviews. The skills test is either in-office on the firm's computer under a time limit (typically 2–4 hours in A.CRE's account) or a take-home exercise over several days, which A.CRE notes is generally harder; you may get a template or have to build from a blank sheet, in Excel and possibly ARGUS. Source: Adventures in CRE, How to Prepare for a Real Estate Technical Interview — https://www.adventuresincre.com/how-to-prepare-for-a-real-estate-technical-interview/
- **Modeling test (Wall Street Prep):** commonly 2–3 hours; the expected tasks are a pro forma built from given assumptions, an amortisation table, a joint-venture waterfall, and a returns summary with sensitivities. A separate case study may follow as a take-home focused on market analysis and an investment thesis, structured as property overview, market positioning, highlights, risks / mitigants, financial highlights, and a recommendation. Source: Wall Street Prep (above)
- **Wharton process contrast:** Wharton MBA Career Management distinguishes a "mature" HR-coordinated process (multiple rounds, fit + industry + mini-case / technical + situational questions) from an "enterprise" process that runs through proactive student-to-alumni outreach and an informational conversation converting into an interview — and warns that the biggest development-interview pitfall alumni named was shallow knowledge of the firm, its properties, and its recent deals. Source: Wharton MBA Career Management, Real Estate Interview Prep Session (Dec 2018, PDF) — https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/RE-Interview-prep-session-dec-18.pdf
- **Brokerage vs. REPE vs. development — no single format:** no fetched firm page (JLL, CBRE, Hines, Eastdil Secured) published an analyst interview format in this pass; firm-process claims for those names are therefore **UNVERIFIED** here. What is sourced is the *difference in emphasis*: development interviewers look for entrepreneurial initiative, project management, negotiation / people skills, and familiarity with the firm's geography and product types, while private-equity / finance interviewers look for quantitative and past-deal experience, business-development and sales skills, and current market deal terms (Wharton, above); brokerage / leasing is defined in the university list as intermediating transactions and representing a tenant or landlord in lease negotiations (university list, §D below).

## A. Technical — valuation & underwriting (10)

1. **Cap rate — define it, compute with it, and say what moves it** — value as income divided by cap rate; back-solve any one of value / income / rate from the other two, and explain why a lower rate signals lower perceived risk and a higher price for the same income. Sources: Adventures in CRE (value equation and risk / return lists in its study outline) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/how-to-prepare-for-a-real-estate-technical-interview/ ; Leveraged Breakdowns, REPE Interview Prep (NOI and cap rates) — https://www.leveragedbreakdowns.com/2020/01/30/repe-interview-prep-what-are-the-main-repe-strategies-explain-noi-and-cap-rates/ ; Leland — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview
2. **Build NOI from the top line** — walk potential gross income through vacancy / credit loss and reimbursements to effective revenue, subtract operating expenses to reach NOI, then continue below the line (leasing / capital costs, then debt service) to cash flow after financing; state what NOI excludes (financing, depreciation, taxes). Sources: Adventures in CRE (the PGI → effective revenue → NOI → cash-flow setup, in paraphrase) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/how-to-prepare-for-a-real-estate-technical-interview/ ; Leland (NOI definition and its use for cap rate, DSCR and cash flow) — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview
3. **The three valuation approaches, and DCF vs. direct capitalisation** — income / cap-rate, comparable sales (per unit or per square foot), and replacement cost used together, with each method's weakness; and when a multi-year discounted cash flow is preferred over capitalising a single stabilised year. Sources: Wall Street Prep (three ways of valuing) — https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/real-estate-private-equity-career-guide/ ; Wharton (three methods of appraising a property) — https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/RE-Interview-prep-session-dec-18.pdf
4. **Going-in cap vs. exit cap** — why the exit rate is normally set at or above entry unless the asset is being repositioned, and what an expansion of the exit rate does to sale proceeds and returns. Sources: Leland (exit-cap discipline and sensitivity discussion) — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview ; Adventures in CRE, Presidio case (sale priced off a stated going-out cap) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/case-study-1-presidio/
5. **Walk through a basic cash-flow pro forma** — rental and other revenue, less vacancy and concessions / abatements, less operating expenses to NOI, less capital items to unlevered cash flow, less financing costs to levered cash flow. Sources: Wall Street Prep (pro forma walkthrough in its top-10) — https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/real-estate-private-equity-career-guide/ ; Adventures in CRE — https://www.adventuresincre.com/how-to-prepare-for-a-real-estate-technical-interview/
6. **Debt metrics — DSCR, debt yield, LTV / LTC, and how a loan is sized** — coverage as income over debt service (below 1.0x means the property does not cover its payments), debt yield as income over loan amount, and leverage as loan over value or cost; a loan is constrained by whichever test binds first. *Loan constant as a named interview prompt: **UNVERIFIED** in this pass — it appears in A.CRE's debt-modeling / amortisation study topics but no fetched page posed it as a standalone question, so it is included here only as part of debt-service fluency, not as an attested prompt.* Sources: Adventures in CRE (DSCR, debt yield and LTV definitions in its basic model) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/how-to-prepare-for-a-real-estate-technical-interview/ ; Leland (DSCR definition and use) — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview ; university common-questions list (debt yield, LTV and DSCR in its technical-definitions list) — https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/74254691-9c6e-41c4-bdff-6f0a27fdb1b4/downloads/Common%20RE%20Interview%20Questions%20updated22523.pdf?ver=1742403539313
7. **IRR vs. equity multiple vs. cash-on-cash — and levered vs. unlevered** — IRR as the time-weighted annualised rate, the multiple as total returned over total invested (ignoring time), and cash-on-cash as a single year's levered cash flow over equity; say when leverage helps and when it only adds risk. Sources: Leland (return-metric definitions, levered vs. unlevered, and its mental-math set) — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview ; Adventures in CRE (IRR, equity multiple, cash-on-cash in its return-metrics study list) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/how-to-prepare-for-a-real-estate-technical-interview/
8. **Mental-math / pen-and-paper drill** — a short priced-at-a-cap, part-levered purchase worked without Excel: derive income, debt cost, equity, and first-year cash-on-cash, then extend it to a multi-year hold for an approximate multiple and IRR. Sources: Wall Street Oasis, Real Estate Interview Questions Master Thread (**community-sourced**) — https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/real-estate/real-estate-interview-questions-master-thread ; Wall Street Prep (its worked cash-on-cash and no-growth IRR prompts in the top-10) — https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/real-estate-private-equity-career-guide/
9. **Sensitivity — what breaks the deal?** — flex rent growth, vacancy / rollover, expenses, purchase price, leverage, and especially the exit cap, and state which assumption the return is most sensitive to and whether the deal still clears its hurdle under a stressed exit. Sources: Leland (sensitivity tables and exit-cap shifts; red flags for aggressive assumptions) — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview ; Adventures in CRE, Windsor Crossing (extra-credit stresses: rents disappoint, costs rise, a tenant is late, exit cap moves) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/real-estate-technical-interview-modeling-test-windsor-crossing/
10. **Leases, tenant risk, and why physically identical buildings are priced differently** — rent roll, tenant credit, diversification, weighted-average lease term, in-place vs. market rent, plus lease structure (e.g. triple-net), expense stops, and the operating-expense vs. capital-expenditure line; and why a stabilised income must not be capitalised as if an unstabilised one were. Sources: Wall Street Prep (identical-buildings prompt in its top-10) — https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/real-estate-private-equity-career-guide/ ; Wall Street Oasis Master Thread (**community-sourced**, same prompt) — https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/real-estate/real-estate-interview-questions-master-thread ; Wharton (triple-net lease, expense stop, FFO / CMBS / leverage-by-property-type in its finance and development lists) — https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/RE-Interview-prep-session-dec-18.pdf ; Adventures in CRE (lease structures and OpEx vs. CapEx in its study outline) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/how-to-prepare-for-a-real-estate-technical-interview/

## B. Modeling-test & case formats (6)

11. **Timed Excel acquisition model** — from a blank file or a template, under a stated time limit, build a simple acquisition / pro forma from given assumptions (rent roll, expenses, financing terms) flowing to IRR and equity multiple, cleanly enough that a reviewer can audit it. Sources: Adventures in CRE, Watch Me Solve a REPE Technical Interview Modeling Test (a blank file, a two-hour window, and an assumptions list, sourced from a WSO-reported test) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/watch-model-real-estate-technical-interview-test/ ; Wall Street Prep (modeling-test task list) — https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/real-estate-private-equity-career-guide/ ; Leland (short modeling tests, in-person or take-home) — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview
12. **Acquisition case with a hurdle — does it clear?** — underwrite a value-add acquisition (lease-up, refinance at stabilisation, sale at an exit cap) and conclude explicitly whether it meets the firm's stated levered-IRR target. Source: Adventures in CRE, Case Study #1 — Presidio (presented by A.CRE as mimicking interview modeling tests) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/case-study-1-presidio/
13. **Development modeling test** — from a blank workbook, project a ground-up scheme month by month: land and closing costs, hard / soft costs and contingency, tenant improvements and leasing commissions, construction financing and its circularity, lease-up, a stabilised hold, and a sale — with sources and uses that balance. Sources: Adventures in CRE, Windsor Crossing (built to mirror a live, timed, in-person analyst exam proctored for a top industrial developer) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/real-estate-technical-interview-modeling-test-windsor-crossing/ ; Adventures in CRE, Watch Me Solve (office-development variant) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/watch-model-real-estate-technical-interview-test/
14. **Debt schedule + JV waterfall / promote build** — build the amortisation / debt-service schedule, then split equity and distributions through a preferred-return hurdle and a promote tier, and report project-level and LP-level returns separately. Sources: Wall Street Prep (amortisation table and JV waterfall in its modeling-test list) — https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/real-estate-private-equity-career-guide/ ; Adventures in CRE, Presidio extra credit (pari-passu to a pref, then a promote) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/case-study-1-presidio/ ; Wharton (JV equity / promote-structure walkthrough in its acquisitions list) — https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/RE-Interview-prep-session-dec-18.pdf
15. **Written investment memo / investment-committee pitch** — a short memo or one-pager that carries the model: overview, market, highlights, risks and mitigants, financials, and a clear recommendation — written as if for a committee that will challenge it. Sources: Wall Street Prep (case-study contents list) — https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/real-estate-private-equity-career-guide/ ; Leland (market memo and write-up-with-model formats) — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview ; Wharton ("if you were preparing a memo for a real estate investment committee, what would you include?") — https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/RE-Interview-prep-session-dec-18.pdf
16. **Cold buy / pass case + present and defend your conclusions** — given a priced asset cold, form a buy-or-pass view from in-place income and cap, the business plan, risks, and financing, then present two or three conclusions backed by numbers and defend them under follow-up. Sources: Leland (buy-vs.-pass and cold multifamily-prompt formats) — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview ; Adventures in CRE (presentation-of-conclusions step: keep it simple, back the obvious conclusions with hard numbers) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/how-to-prepare-for-a-real-estate-technical-interview/

## C. Market & deal talk (7)

17. **Why this market / submarket?** — argue a market from rents, vacancy, absorption, comparable trades, and the supply pipeline / deliveries, not from national headlines; REPE is presented in the sources as a local, asset-level game. Sources: Leland (market judgment; submarket fundamentals and supply / demand) — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview ; Wharton (risks of entering a new market; most / least promising sector) — https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/RE-Interview-prep-session-dec-18.pdf
18. **Compare property types on cap rate and risk** — rank the main types and defend the ranking from lease length, operational intensity, tenant credit, and cyclicality (e.g. nightly-stay hospitality at the risky end, multifamily at the defensive end, in the sources' ordering). Sources: Wall Street Prep (cap-rate / risk comparison in its top-10, and its property-type table) — https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/real-estate-private-equity-career-guide/ ; Wall Street Oasis Master Thread (**community-sourced**, hotel vs. multifamily cap prompt) — https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/real-estate/real-estate-interview-questions-master-thread
19. **The strategy spectrum — core, core-plus, value-add, opportunistic** — place a deal on the spectrum and match it to its risk drivers (occupancy, lease term, tenant credit, deferred maintenance, leverage, development) and its return ambition. Sources: Wall Street Prep (four strategies in its top-10) — https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/real-estate-private-equity-career-guide/ ; Leveraged Breakdowns (same four strategies, asset quality vs. target return inverse) — https://www.leveragedbreakdowns.com/2020/01/30/repe-interview-prep-what-are-the-main-repe-strategies-explain-noi-and-cap-rates/
20. **"If you had a large pool of equity, where would you put it — and what return would you expect?"** — a specific market / property-type thesis with a return range that is plausible for that strategy, showing you follow the news and have a view. Sources: Wall Street Oasis Master Thread (**community-sourced**) — https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/real-estate/real-estate-interview-questions-master-thread ; Wharton (invest-$100M prompt in its market-knowledge list) — https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/RE-Interview-prep-session-dec-18.pdf ; university common-questions list (invest-money prompt in its behavioural set) — https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/74254691-9c6e-41c4-bdff-6f0a27fdb1b4/downloads/Common%20RE%20Interview%20Questions%20updated22523.pdf?ver=1742403539313
21. **Walk me through a deal — one you worked on, or a mock / self-built one** — in investor order: asset and market, underwriting assumptions, capital structure, value-creation plan, exit and sensitivities, your own role, and what you would do differently; entry-level candidates are expected to substitute a self-built underwriting if they have no live deal. Sources: Leland (deal walkthrough structure; mock-underwriting starter for candidates without a live deal) — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview ; Wall Street Oasis Master Thread (**community-sourced**, deal walkthrough with "would you have invested?") — https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/real-estate/real-estate-interview-questions-master-thread
22. **Development judgement — build vs. buy, land price, and yield on cost** — whether a scheme pencils: stabilised yield on total cost against the exit cap (the development spread), what you can pay for land given density / FAR, and how construction-cost inflation and construction-loan terms squeeze the spread. *Spread levels quoted in the WSO thread are dated, community-reported figures — the prompt type is attested, any specific basis-point level is **UNVERIFIED / time-specific**.* Sources: Wall Street Oasis, Yield on Development Costs (**community-sourced**; spread defined there as yield on cost minus exit cap) — https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/real-estate/yield-on-development-costs ; Wharton (FAR, and how to decide what to pay for development land) — https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/RE-Interview-prep-session-dec-18.pdf ; Adventures in CRE, Windsor Crossing (development budget, construction financing, merchant-build sale) — https://www.adventuresincre.com/real-estate-technical-interview-modeling-test-windsor-crossing/
23. **Rates and the cycle — what does a higher cost of capital do to your underwriting?** — connect rates to cap / discount rates, loan proceeds and refinancing risk, why development stops pencilling, and why a disciplined answer underwrites to today's cost of capital rather than hoping for rate relief. Sources: ULI / Urban Land, Economic Snapshot: A New Cost-of-Capital Reality for Commercial Real Estate (Sept 2026; practitioner views on higher cap / discount rates, debt and selective value-add / opportunistic, and income growth over rate bets) — https://urbanland.uli.org/capital-markets-and-finance/a-new-cost-of-capital-reality-for-commercial-real-estate ; Leland (leverage, exit and downside discussion in deal cases) — https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/real-estate-private-equity-interview

## Standardisation note

Real estate is **moderately standardised on technicals, weakly standardised on process**: the *technical core* (cap rate, NOI build, DCF / pro forma, DSCR / debt yield / LTV, IRR vs. multiple vs. cash-on-cash, sensitivities) and the *modeling-test shape* (timed Excel pro forma + amortisation + waterfall + returns / sensitivity summary, then a memo or buy / pass defence) recur independently across A.CRE, Wall Street Prep, Leland, Wharton, and the university list. The *process around it* (round count, timed vs. take-home, brokerage vs. REPE vs. development emphasis, and whether a superday ends with a test) varies by firm, track, and entry point, and no firm-published universal format was verified in this pass.
