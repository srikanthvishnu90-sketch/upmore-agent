## Principles
- A referral works when it is easy to give: the contact can forward one short message and a resume, and it takes them two minutes.
- Ask people who know the person's work first. Weak ties (alumni, acquaintances) help when the ask is specific and the reason is honest.
- Do not ask for a referral from someone who has not seen the person's work; ask for advice first and the referral can follow.
## Steps
- Pick one specific opening and read its description.
- Find people connected to the employer: classmates, alumni, past coworkers, professors, and friends of friends.
- Ask for a short call first when you do not know them. After the call, ask whether they would be comfortable referring.
- Send a forwardable note: the role link, two lines on why the person fits, and the resume attached.
- Thank them, then tell them what happened.
- If no one is connected, apply and send a short message to the hiring manager or team lead as well.
## Ask
- Ask which role and employer they are targeting and whether they know anyone there.
## Do not claim
- Do not promise a referral will produce an interview.
- Do not name referral bonuses or rules.
