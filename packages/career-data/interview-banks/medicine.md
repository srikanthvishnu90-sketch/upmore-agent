# Medicine — Interview Question Bank (med-school admissions + residency)

Count: **30 canonical items** (6 MMI station types + 8 traditional admissions prompts + 13 residency prompts in 7 AAMC categories + 3 CASPer tasks).*

## Format facts (sourced)

### Med-school admissions — MMI
- The Multiple Mini Interview was developed at McMaster University; the AAMC describes it as 6–10 stations, each on a different question/scenario, measuring communication, social/non-verbal skills and teamwork. Source: AAMC Students & Residents, What It's Like to Participate in MMIs — https://students-residents.aamc.org/applying-medical-school/what-it-s-participate-multiple-mini-interviews-mmis
- Timing (AAMC): the circuit runs nearly two hours; each station has a 2-minute prep period, then a 5–8 minute conversation. Source: AAMC (same page). Mercer University's MMI guide gives the same ranges (6–10 stations, 2 min reading, 6–10 min per station, ~1.5–2.5 hours total). Source: Mercer University / Inspira Advantage MMI guide — https://career.mercer.edu/blog/2026/09/29/admissions-expert-on-how-to-ace-med-school-mmi-interviews-sample-qas/
- Why schools use it: multiple independent raters limit single-interviewer bias; one weak station does not decide the outcome. Sources: AAMC (same page); Student Doctor Network interview-styles overview (each station standardised and scored independently) — https://www.studentdoctor.net/2026/01/29/understanding-different-medical-school-interview-styles/
- The MMI does not test specific medical knowledge; it tests interpersonal and critical-thinking skills, and interviewers care more about how you reason than your final answer. Source: AAMC (same page)

### CASPer
- CASPer is an open-response situational judgment test, originally developed in 2004 at McMaster for medical admissions, now run by Acuity Insights and used by 600+ programs. Source: Acuity Insights — https://acuityinsights.com/resources/research/casper-is-the-most-widely-used-situational-judgment-test-in-admissions/
- Structure (describing Acuity's published format via Collegiate Gateway's summary of it): 14 scenarios — video-response section first (30 seconds' reflection, up to 1 minute per recorded answer, two questions per scenario), then a typed section (30 seconds to think, 5 minutes for three questions per scenario); total 90–110 minutes with two optional breaks; scenarios need not be medical. Source: Collegiate Gateway, Casper and Duet — https://collegiategateway.com/medical-school-interviews-part-i-casper-and-duet/
- Canadian Medical Association's explainer gives a somewhat different current count (11 scenarios, 65–85 minutes) — **formats have changed across cycles; treat exact scenario counts as cycle-specific and verify on Acuity's site for your test date.** Source: CMA, What is the Casper test? (search-verified) — https://www.cma.ca/healthcare-for-real/what-casper-test
- Scoring: each scenario is scored by a different trained rater (to limit bias); spelling does not affect the typed score; programs receive percentile / standardised scores. Source: Collegiate Gateway (above)

### Residency interviews
- There is no single national residency interview format: AAMC states specific questions vary by program, but fall into the seven categories in §C below. Source: AAMC Students & Residents, Common Residency Interview Topics — https://students-residents.aamc.org/applying-residencies-eras/publication-chapters/common-residency-interview-topics
- Candidates are expected to arrive with a long list of their own questions, directed appropriately (program-performance questions to the program director; support/culture questions to residents) — and AAMC advises not asking about salary/benefits/vacation in the interview. Source: AAMC, Evaluating and Choosing a Residency (search-verified) — https://students-residents.aamc.org/applying-residencies-eras/publication-chapters/evaluating-and-choosing-residency

## A. MMI station types (6) — AAMC-attested

1. **Ethical scenario station** — a dilemma with social/policy implications (e.g., competing principles: patient autonomy vs. doing good vs. fair use of scarce resources); discuss reasoning, don't hunt for a "right" answer. Sources: AAMC MMI page (ethical scenario station, virtual and in-person lists) — https://students-residents.aamc.org/applying-medical-school/what-it-s-participate-multiple-mini-interviews-mmis ; Mercer MMI guide (ethical framework: autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice) — https://career.mercer.edu/blog/2026/09/29/admissions-expert-on-how-to-ace-med-school-mmi-interviews-sample-qas/
2. **Actor / standardised-patient role-play station** — interact with an actor (e.g., an upset or confused patient/family member); scored on empathy, listening and communication, not medical facts. Sources: AAMC MMI page; SDN interview-styles overview (role-play e.g. delivering bad news) — https://www.studentdoctor.net/2026/01/29/understanding-different-medical-school-interview-styles/
3. **Teamwork station** — complete a task with another candidate (at Duke, described as paired instruction-giving / instruction-following rotations); scored on collaboration and communication. Sources: AAMC MMI page; Collegiate Gateway MMI summary of Duke's format — https://collegiategateway.com/medical-school-interviews-part-iv-mmi/
4. **Traditional one-on-one station inside the MMI** — a short conventional interview with an admissions officer/faculty member (motivation, experiences, fit for that school's mission). Source: AAMC MMI page
5. **Essay / writing station** — a timed written response; AAMC notes it may run longer than other stations. Source: AAMC MMI page
6. **Critical-thinking / current-events / policy station** — discuss a health-policy or current-events prompt, or interpret a scenario/video and propose solutions; schools tailor these to their mission. Sources: AAMC MMI page (critical thinking, current events, role-play emphasis vary by school); Duke station mix as summarised by Collegiate Gateway (scenario stations, a video station, traditional and team stations) — https://collegiategateway.com/medical-school-interviews-part-iv-mmi/
   - *Not scored:* AAMC also lists a **rest station** — a format feature, not a question type, so it is not counted above.

**What MMI stations assess (Duke, via Collegiate Gateway's summary of Duke's own description):** empathy; initiative and resilience; communication and problem-solving; teamwork; insight and integrity; cultural competence. Source: https://collegiategateway.com/medical-school-interviews-part-iv-mmi/

## D. CASPer tasks (3)

28. **Video-response scenario** — watch/read a dilemma (often non-medical, e.g. a workplace/family conflict), reflect ~30 seconds, record up to 1-minute answers to two questions about what the person should do and why. Source: Collegiate Gateway summary of Acuity's format — https://collegiategateway.com/medical-school-interviews-part-i-casper-and-duet/
29. **Typed-response scenario** — after a video or written scenario, 5 minutes to answer three open questions explaining your course of action and reasoning. Source: Collegiate Gateway (above)
30. **Competencies sampled across scenarios** — collaboration, communication, empathy, equity/fairness, ethics, motivation, problem-solving, professionalism, resilience, self-awareness (Acuity's own list as reproduced in its rater job postings). Source: Acuity Insights rater posting summary in search results — https://jobs.updata.com/companies/acuity-insights/jobs/39416939-casper-test-rater-casual-contract-work-remote-in-australia ; CMA explainer (overlapping competency list) — https://www.cma.ca/healthcare-for-real/what-casper-test

## Worked MMI example (ORIGINAL station built for this bank, in the canonical ethics format)

**Station prompt (original):** *You are a medical student on a ward round. Afterwards, a competent adult patient tells you privately that she does not want her adult children told her new diagnosis yet, and asks you to make sure of it. Her son then approaches you in the corridor and asks what the diagnosis is. You have 2 minutes to prepare, then 6 minutes to discuss what you would do.*

**A strong response, step by step:**
1. **Name the tension:** the patient's confidentiality/autonomy vs. the son's understandable concern and the family's role in care.
2. **Map the stakeholders:** patient (decision-maker), son, the treating team (who owns the conversation — as a student you escalate, you do not decide alone), other family.
3. **Act in the corridor:** do not confirm or deny the diagnosis; acknowledge the son's worry empathetically; explain you can't share another adult's health information without her permission; offer a route — ask the patient, with the team, whether she'd like a family conversation and support to have it.
4. **Escalate correctly:** tell the supervising doctor about both conversations the same day; document per team direction.
5. **Add nuance / follow-up readiness:** if the patient lacked capacity, or there were a safety risk to others, the balance changes — say what extra information would change your approach.

**Scoring criteria (how such stations are actually scored):**
- Each station is scored **independently by its own rater**, and station scores are aggregated — one bad station is diluted by the others. Sources: AAMC MMI page; SDN — https://www.studentdoctor.net/2026/01/29/understanding-different-medical-school-interview-styles/
- McMaster-linked guidance summarised by Student Doctor Network describes raters grading **communication skills, strength of the arguments, and suitability for the profession**. Source: SDN, Top 8 Strategies to Prepare for the MMI (search-verified) — https://www.studentdoctor.net/2017/08/30/multiple-mini-interview-mmi/
- Mercer/Inspira's rubric summary adds the within-station dimensions: **problem identification, stakeholder analysis, logical reasoning, communication** — plus qualitative rater comments reviewed alongside the numeric score. Source: Mercer MMI guide — https://career.mercer.edu/blog/2026/09/29/admissions-expert-on-how-to-ace-med-school-mmi-interviews-sample-qas/
- Applied to the example above: full marks require naming the principle conflict (not just "keep it secret"), covering all stakeholders, taking a clear position, communicating it empathetically, and knowing the limits of a student's role.

## Standardisation note

Medicine is the **most standardised** of the three families — by design. The MMI is a patented-style, research-backed format with fixed station counts, fixed prep/response windows, independent raters and rubrics, deliberately built (at McMaster) to be more reliable and less biased than a single long interview; CASPer extends the same philosophy into a timed, centrally scored test. Residency interviewing, by contrast, is standardised only at the category level — AAMC publishes the seven topic categories, but programs write their own questions.
