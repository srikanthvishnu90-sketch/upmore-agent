## Format facts (sourced)

- **Typical funnel (US analyst / summer analyst):** application → recorded first round (often HireVue) or campus/phone first round → Superday final round. WSP describes the Superday as the final step before offers for internships and on-cycle analyst/associate roles, with back-to-back interviews with bankers from analyst to senior level. Source: WSP-Superday.
- **First round length:** OU Career Center describes a first round of roughly 30–45 minutes mixing behavioral and technical questions. Source: OU-Career.
- **Goldman Sachs pattern (one firm, not universal):** Leland describes two main stages for IBD — a recorded HireVue, then a Superday of typically three sessions of about 20–30 minutes each with analysts, associates and VPs, with no separate recruiter screen / assessment / written case in between for most candidates. Source: Leland-GS.
- **Superday breadth:** FE Training describes a superday as several back-to-back interviews in one day, usually three to six, covering commercial awareness, technical knowledge, and competency/fit, with offers often following within days. Source: FE-Superday.
- **Who asks what:** WSP notes junior analysts tend to ask the hardest technical questions, senior bankers lean behavioral/background — a pattern, not a rule; candidates should expect curveballs in any seat. Source: WSP-Superday.
- **Timed vs untimed:** The HireVue stage is timed per-answer (prep + record window, single-take at some firms — Leland-GS advises practising on a ~2-minute timer for GS). Live technical walk-throughs (DCF, paper LBO, merger math) are untimed in clock terms but judged on fluency under follow-up — M&I and WSP both frame them as spoken walk-throughs, not written exams. Paper LBOs are explicitly described as live, pen-and-paper, no computer/calculator in PE-style interviews. Source: PaperLBO-OH. Precise HireVue prep/record seconds vary by firm — **UNVERIFIED as a single universal number; do not teach one number as universal.**
- **EMEA difference:** M&I notes EMEA candidates may face an assessment centre instead of a US-style Superday. Source: M&I (via its IB vs S&T comparison, fetched in search).

### Model answer — $10 increase in depreciation (assume 20% tax for clean arithmetic; state your tax assumption first — many guides use 30% or 40%, the mechanics are identical)

- **Income statement:** Depreciation expense +$10 → pre-tax income −$10 → tax −$2 (at 20%) → **net income −$8**.
- **Cash flow statement:** Start with net income −$8, add back depreciation +$10 (non-cash) → **cash from operations +$2**. No investing/financing effect. Cash rises +$2.
- **Balance sheet:** Cash +$2; PP&E (net, via accumulated depreciation) −$10 → assets −$8. Retained earnings −$8 (from lower net income) → liabilities + equity −$8. **Balances.**
- Check yourself: the only real cash effect is the tax shield, +$10 × tax rate. If the interviewer says "assume no taxes," then net income −$10, CFO $0, cash $0, PP&E −$10, retained earnings −$10.

### REQUIRED — Enterprise value vs equity value (model answer)

- **Equity value** = value to common shareholders only = diluted share price × diluted shares outstanding.
- **Enterprise value** = value of the core operating business to *all* capital providers.
- **Bridge (equity → EV):** EV = Equity Value + Total Debt + Preferred Stock + Non-controlling (minority) Interest + other debt-like claims (e.g. finance leases, underfunded pension, where applicable) − Cash & Equivalents − non-operating assets.
- **Bridge (EV → equity):** reverse it: Equity Value = EV − Debt − Preferred − NCI − debt-like claims + Cash + non-operating assets; then ÷ diluted shares = implied share price.
- **Why it matters in one line:** you add claims you must assume/refinance to buy the whole business, and subtract cash/non-operating assets the buyer effectively gets back. Pair EV numerators only with unlevered denominators, equity numerators only with levered denominators (see item 26).
- Sources: WSP-Superday; WSP-Top10.

### REQUIRED — "Walk me through a DCF" — full model-answer outline

State first: "I'll do an unlevered DCF — the most common version — which values the firm to all capital providers."

1. **Project unlevered free cash flow (UFCF / FCFF), typically 5–10 years.**
   - Project revenue (growth), margins → EBIT.
   - UFCF = EBIT × (1 − tax rate) + Depreciation & Amortisation − Change in Net Working Capital − Capital Expenditures.
   - Say what drives each line; do not skip working capital or capex.
2. **Build WACC (the discount rate).**
   - WACC = (E/(D+E)) × Cost of Equity + (D/(D+E)) × After-tax Cost of Debt, using market values.
   - Cost of Equity via CAPM = Risk-free rate (commonly the 10-year US Treasury yield) + Beta × Equity Risk Premium.
   - Beta: unlever comparable betas, average, relever at the target's capital structure: Unlevered β = Levered β / [1 + (1−T)(D/E)]; reverse to relever.
   - Cost of debt: yield on the firm's debt / risk-free + spread, tax-effected because interest is deductible.
3. **Terminal value — give BOTH methods and cross-check them.**
   - *Gordon growth / perpetuity:* TV = Final-year UFCF × (1 + g) / (WACC − g), with g a modest long-run rate (cannot exceed long-run economy growth indefinitely).
   - *Exit multiple:* TV = Final-year EBITDA (or EBIT) × EV/EBITDA exit multiple from comps.
   - Note TV is often the majority of EV — flag that as the DCF's main sensitivity/weakness.
4. **Discount to present value.**
   - PV = Σ UFCF_t / (1+WACC)^t + TV / (1+WACC)^n. Mention mid-year convention if used (cash arrives through the year; exponents shift by 0.5) and stub period if relevant.
5. **EV → equity bridge → share price.**
   - Sum of PV(UFCF) + PV(TV) = Enterprise Value. Subtract net debt (debt − cash) and non-equity claims (NCI, preferred) → Equity Value. Divide by **diluted** shares → implied share price. Compare to current price.
6. **Sensitivity / sanity check — always close with this.**
   - Sensitise WACC vs terminal growth (and WACC vs exit multiple), plus revenue growth vs margin. State which assumption the answer lives or dies on.
- Sources for this structure: WSP-DCF (forecast FCFF → TV by both approaches → discount at WACC → EV-to-equity → diluted share price → sensitivity); M&I (unlevered DCF walk-through with the same UFCF formula and both TV methods).

## D. M&A / Merger models — 7 items

31. **Goodwill — what is it, why is it created? Purchase-accounting adjustments, DTA/DTL creation, NOLs in models.** Source: WSP-Superday.
32. **Who pays more — strategic or financial buyer — and why?** Source: WSP-Superday.
33. **Cash vs debt vs stock consideration — differences and EPS consequences.** Source: WSP-Superday.
34. **Asset sale vs stock sale — why buyers generally prefer asset sales; §338(h)(10) at awareness level.** Source: WSP-Superday. *Detail beyond the prompt is UNVERIFIED here — teach the prompt and the headline reason (basis step-up / tax) only with a tax caveat.*

### REQUIRED — Merger accretion / dilution (model answer)

- A deal is **accretive** if the acquirer's pro-forma EPS rises vs standalone EPS; **dilutive** if it falls. Compute: Combined EPS = Combined Net Income ÷ Combined Shares; % accretion = Combined EPS / Standalone EPS − 1.
- Combined net income adjustments: + target net income, − after-tax interest on new debt, − after-tax foregone interest on cash used, − incremental D&A from asset write-ups / new intangibles, + after-tax synergies. Combined shares: + new shares issued in a stock deal.
- **Rule of thumb (100% stock only):** if acquirer P/E > P/E paid for target (at the offer price, incl. premium), accretive; if lower, dilutive. Logic: cost of stock = 1 / acquirer P/E (its earnings yield); seller's yield = 1 / P/E paid. Cost < yield → accretive.
- **Mixed consideration:** compare the *weighted* cost — after-tax cost of cash, after-tax cost of debt, and 1/(acquirer P/E) for the stock portion, weighted by mix — to the seller's yield at the purchase price. Weighted cost < seller's yield → accretive.
- Worked check (from BIWS-Merger, paraphrased numbers): buyer EPS $1.00 (NI $10, 10 shares); buys target (NI $10) for $150 equity value in stock at $25/share → issues 6 shares → combined NI $20 / 16 shares = $1.25 → **25% accretive**.
- Caveat to volunteer: EPS accretion is not value creation — a deal can be accretive and still destroy value.
- Sources: BIWS-Merger; WSP-Superday.

### REQUIRED — Paper LBO — full model-answer outline

Format fact first: given a pen, paper and a set of assumptions live in the interview; no computer/calculator. Source: PaperLBO-OH.

1. **State assumptions out loud before calculating** (entry EBITDA, entry multiple, leverage multiple, fees if given, hold period — typically 5 years, EBITDA growth, interest rate, tax rate, capex/D&A/working capital if given, debt paydown assumption, exit multiple). Narrating assumptions lets the interviewer redirect you early — that is part of the test.
2. **Entry / Sources & Uses.**
   - Entry EV = Entry EBITDA × Entry Multiple.
   - Debt = Leverage Multiple × Entry EBITDA (split into tranches only if given).
   - Uses = Entry EV (equity purchase price, adjusting for assumed/refinanced debt and cash acquired if given) + transaction fees + financing fees.
   - Sources = Debt + Rollover (if any) + Sponsor Equity (the plug: Uses − Debt − Rollover). Sponsor equity = entry equity check.
3. **Project the hold period (simple, stated simplifications are allowed).**
   - Grow EBITDA by the given growth path. Build levered FCF each year in this chain: EBITDA − Interest − Taxes − Capex − Change in Working Capital = Levered FCF. (If D&A is given, taxes are on EBIT after interest; if not, state your simplified tax base.)
   - Recompute interest as debt falls if you sweep debt — or state "interest on beginning balance" as an explicit simplification.
4. **Debt schedule.**
   - Mandatory amortisation first (if given), then apply levered FCF as an optional sweep / cash paydown (standard paper-LBO assumption: all FCF pays down debt unless told otherwise). Track the running debt balance year by year; any FCF not used to pay debt accumulates as cash.
5. **Exit.**
   - Exit EV = Exit-year EBITDA × Exit Multiple (often assumed = entry multiple; say so).
   - Exit Equity Value = Exit EV − Remaining Debt + Cash accumulated.
   - **Never forget this bridge** — using exit EV as equity value is the classic penalised error.
6. **Returns.**
   - MoM / MOIC = Exit Equity ÷ Entry Equity.
   - IRR from MoM and hold length: memorised 5-year anchors — 2.0x ≈ 15%, 2.5x ≈ 20%, 3.0x ≈ 25% (Rule of 72 for doubling; Rule of 114 ≈ tripling). Interpolate and state it as approximate.
7. **Sensitivities / attribution — close with this.**
   - Attribute return to its three sources: EBITDA growth, multiple expansion (zero if exit = entry), and debt paydown. Sensitise exit multiple ±1 turn and entry multiple +1 turn / growth lower — returns fall fastest on entry price.
- Worked standard example (numbers from PaperLBO-10x, paraphrased): $100 EBITDA bought at 10x = $1,000 EV; 6.0x debt = $600; entry equity $400. EBITDA grows to $161 in year 5; levered FCF accumulates $129 cash; no debt repaid in that variant. Exit at 10x × $161 = $1,610 EV − $600 debt + $129 cash = $1,139 exit equity. MoM = $1,139 / $400 ≈ 2.8x → IRR ≈ 23% over 5 years.
- Sources: PaperLBO-10x; PaperLBO-OH.
