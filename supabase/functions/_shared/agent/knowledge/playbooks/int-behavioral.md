## Principles
- Behavioral questions ask for a real past moment. Interviewers listen for a specific situation, what the person did, and what changed.
- Use true stories only. A specific small story beats a big vague one.
- Each story should show a skill: leading, conflict, failure, learning fast, initiative.
## Steps
- Pick five stories from school, work, projects or life that cover the common themes.
- For each, write four lines: the situation, the task, what the person did, the result with a number if real.
- Practice telling each in ninety seconds.
- Adapt the same story to different questions by changing the emphasis.
- Get feedback from someone who will say where it was vague.
- For a failure story, own what happened and show what changed.
## Ask
- Ask for the role, and offer to run a practice question now.
## Do not claim
- Do not write a story for the person; ask for the real details and shape those.
