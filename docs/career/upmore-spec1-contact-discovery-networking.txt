======================================================================
0. DECISION AND HONEST LIMITS
======================================================================

Recommended path: a shortlist-first hybrid. Find names and relevance using permitted public company/team/job sources and user-approved relationships. Rank before paying to reveal emails. Use one commercially approved name/domain email finder plus verification. Add paid people search only when discovering the right people is demonstrably the bottleneck. Show evidence, verification time and limitations. Prepare short truthful outreach and require review of the recipient and final words before sending.

The easy initial product is not an unrestricted LinkedIn robot. It is a useful research assistant and relationship tracker with a clean enrichment interface. This can be implemented before an expensive embedded-data contract is signed. A broad multi-user contact product requires commercial rights, privacy work, security work and provider approval; buying a $49 subscription is not permission to expose its database to every Upmore customer.

A list of 500 names is not 500 correct current employees, 500 people connected to the job, 500 verified work inboxes, 500 receptive humans or 500 referrals. These are separate counts. Expose them. Never fill a requested list with weak matches just to hit a number. If 187 of 500 requested people meet the quality bar, return 187 and explain the coverage gap.

The user's statement that Muse can do this is a capability goal, not proof of Muse's private data sources, data rights, accuracy or costs. In this conversation Muse is a builder. Do not silently reinterpret it as The Muse career website. The public The Muse pages describe career advice and networking coaching, not a documented universal professional-contact API [S16]. Jobright does publicly advertise insider connections, including alumni, classmates, prior colleagues and hiring managers [S17]. Its advertised 4x interview figure is a marketing claim, not independent proof and not a forecast Upmore may reuse.

Nobody can honestly promise every hiring manager's current email, exhaustive coverage of all jobs, or a legal guarantee from a data vendor's marketing page. A contact database can be stale. A verifier can report a mailbox as deliverable without confirming its owner's identity. A recipient can ignore good outreach. Contact discovery is one input to hiring, not a substitute for eligibility, relevant work, interviewing or an actual opening.

======================================================================
1. WHAT THE PRODUCT MUST DO
======================================================================

Core user requests:
- Find people relevant to a specific job, company, office, function or industry.
- Identify alumni and other real relationship bridges without inventing familiarity.
- Find recruiters responsible for the relevant role family, not any recruiter with the company name.
- Find plausible hiring-team employees, clearly separating confirmed hiring ownership from inference.
- Build a large research list with provenance, current-role checks, duplicates removed and costs controlled.
- Prepare useful networking messages and track responses without acting as a spam service.
- Notify the user when a relevant opening appears and explain who might be worth contacting.
- Suggest a job-specific proof-of-work project that makes a conversation worth having.

Non-goals for the first release:
- Automated LinkedIn browsing, connection requests, DMs or profile scraping.
- Bulk extraction from restricted alumni directories.
- Purchasing, reselling or centrally exposing personal phone/home-address data.
- Inferring protected characteristics, immigration status or intimate personal details from public profiles.
- Claiming someone can refer the user without evidence of eligibility and willingness.
- Writing flattering but unsupported claims about a person's work.
- Real sends, applications, referrals or account changes without permission.

Every contact card must answer:
1. Who is this person and which identity evidence supports the match?
2. Why are they relevant to this company, job, office or career question?
3. What is confirmed, what is stale, and what is only inferred?
4. Which permitted route can the user use to contact them?
5. What information would be disclosed and from which sending account?
6. What is the cost already incurred and the cost of the next step?

The default experience should be simple: job or company in, three to five best contacts out, with one honest reason each. Large-list mode exists, but it must not force the user to manually audit a noisy spreadsheet.

2. MOST EFFICIENT WORKABLE ROUTE AND COST COMPARISON
======================================================================

Rank by usable verified contact, not by cheap API call. Define a usable contact as a unique, current, relevant professional with a permitted contact route, identity evidence, recent mailbox verification where applicable and no suppression condition. Legal basis and license rights are gates, not bonus points in a ranking.

Recommended operational sequence:
A. Canonicalize company, role, office and domain.
B. Read the employer's actual job page and permitted team/career pages.
C. Check user-approved existing contacts and explicit relationship bridges.
D. Find and rank names from those sources before any email purchase.
E. Buy name/domain finding only for the shortlist, under approved provider terms.
F. Verify shortlisted work emails. Hold catch-all and unknown results separately.
G. Show evidence-backed cards and prepare outreach for review.
H. Expand through an approved people-search provider only when name discovery fails.
I. Measure actual usable yield and only then increase volume.

Comparative ranking:

1. Existing user-approved relationships + public employer pages
Cost: no enrichment charge when an employer already publishes a relevant email, but search, parsing and labor are not free.
Coverage: uneven. Excellent for named campus recruiters, public staff, research labs, small firms and known contacts; weak for private hiring ownership at large employers.
Speed: fast on clean team pages, slower when pages are vague or restricted.
Risk: lower than unauthorized platform extraction, but still check website rights, privacy basis and permitted use of user's contacts.
Best use: initial names, direct recruiter routes, relationship warmth and evidence.

2. Name/domain finder + verifier after ranking, proposed first paid adapter: Hunter
Observed all-in-one Starter list price: $49/month for 2,000 credits; annual equivalent displayed as $34/month billed $408 yearly [S2]. Do not buy an annual commitment just to test. The same page has a separate Data Platform option, so obtain a quote for the actual API/product use rather than assuming the displayed outreach plan is an embedded-data license.
Observed credit rules: one credit for a found Email Finder result; 0.5 for a verification; regular Domain Search one credit per email, bulk domain search up to ten emails per credit [S2]. Endpoint availability and billable rules must be checked again in the selected account.
Nominal allocation for finder + verifier at full use: $49 / 2,000 * 1.5 = $0.03675 per successful found-and-verified email. This is not the cost per correct relevant contact. It excludes identity rejects, undeliverable/catch-all outcomes, minimum subscription waste, people discovery, license fees, search/model costs, taxes and operating work.
Coverage: good when name and correct company domain are known; not a substitute for discovering the right hiring team.
Speed: API batch/queue workflow is easy; verification and source coverage vary.
Risk: manageable only with written approval when Upmore's feature is considered similar/competitive. Hunter's terms prohibit building a similar or competitive service without prior written consent [S3].
Verdict: simplest paid technical path after rights approval; use source-backed emails and reject risky outputs.

3. Approved Apollo people search + selected enrichment
Search endpoint documented as zero credits, but it does not return email or phone details [S4]. Enrichment is separately billed; current docs describe 1-9 credits per person without waterfall, with email/demographics and phone components [S5, S6]. Credits are not dollars and no Upmore embedded-use dollar quote was obtained.
Coverage: broader professional discovery and title/company filtering; hiring ownership remains unproven.
Speed: likely operationally efficient through batch APIs, but actual latency/yield not benchmarked here.
Risk: high if implemented under an ordinary account without commercial permission. Apollo explicitly limits the API to internal business purposes and requires authorization for third-party credentials or product integration [S7]. A bring-your-own-key button does not bypass that restriction.
Verdict: strongest candidate for broad name discovery after partner approval; cannot honestly label it the cheapest legal route without a product agreement and measured yield.

4. Approved People Data Labs person search/enrichment
Observed self-serve starting price: $98/month with as few as 350 credits; free 100-credit testing has obfuscated contact data [S8]. Person Search charges one credit per returned record, not per useful correct match [S9].
Nominal full-use allocation at that starting tier: $98 / 350 = $0.28 per returned profile, before separate work verification, rejects and licensing. At 50% usable yield the allocated profile cost doubles to $0.56. This is illustrative arithmetic, not a vendor quote or measured yield.
Coverage: useful professional datasets and structured filters; query result order is profile completeness, not relevance to this user's job [S9].
Speed: documented default person-search rate limit 10 requests/minute; size 1-100 per request [S9]. Do not assume all accounts have that limit or that a full page is worth buying.
Risk: negotiate appropriate product/data rights and permissible use; bulk license documentation explicitly calls for use-case vetting [S10].
Verdict: consider when approved embedding and broad structured discovery matter more than the cheapest starting plan.

5. Approved RocketReach as fallback
Public API page describes person/company/email enrichment; custom packages start at $6K but the page does not establish a universal low-end embedded-use price [S11]. Its terms restrict commercial reproduction, resale and reuse without written consent [S12].
Coverage: vendor advertises broad database reach; no independent coverage benchmark collected.
Speed: API workflow exists; production latency not tested.
Risk: signed rights needed for Upmore's actual use and onward display/export.
Verdict: obtain a quote only after the primary path has a measured coverage gap. Do not buy several overlapping vendors before proving the gap.

6. Unauthorized LinkedIn or alumni-directory scraping
Cost: apparently cheap per extraction, potentially costly account restriction, legal disputes and contaminated data.
Coverage: attractive surface appearance, inaccessible or stale private fields, no guaranteed work email.
Speed: fragile, throttled and challenge-prone.
Risk: unacceptable as the default launch route. LinkedIn explicitly prohibits scraping and unauthorized automated activity [S13, S14]. Stanford directory use also has explicit restrictions [S20].
Verdict: do not build this as the workaround when legitimate data costs money.

Search infrastructure:
Brave's current Search plan advertises $5/1,000 requests [S18]. Twenty targeted searches have a nominal $0.10 request cost before all other costs; this does not imply twenty contacts. API search result storage requires a plan granting storage rights, and search access gives no rights to third-party webpage content [S18].
Google Custom Search JSON API is closed to new customers; existing customers must transition by January 1, 2027 [S19]. Do not copy an old tutorial promising a new integration with its old free tier.

The actual cheapest route depends on coverage and rejection rate. For company pages with published relevant emails it is direct-source research. For known names without emails it may be finder plus verifier. For unknown names across many large companies it may be commercially approved people search plus selective enrichment. One provider cannot win all cases.

If forced to pick one path today: build the public/relationship shortlist-first workflow and one rights-approved Hunter-compatible finder/verifier adapter, with people-search as an optional second-stage interface. That is the simplest workable product shape. Keep live enrichment disabled until written product rights and spend authority exist. This is a recommendation, not a claim that an approved Hunter contract has already been obtained.

3. DISCOVERY METHODS BY SOURCE
======================================================================

Employer-first: start from the employer's verified domain, not a logo or arbitrary search hit. Read permitted career pages, job description contacts, team pages, campus recruiting pages, professional blogs, author biographies and official events. A recruiter named on the requisition is stronger evidence than a generic database title. A team page establishes employment, not hiring ownership.

Canonicalize parent companies, subsidiaries, office and staffing agency relationships. Keep contact employer and hiring employer separate. A recruiter employed by a staffing firm must not be represented as a client company's employee. The applicant tracking domain is not necessarily the employee email domain.

Search recipe: company + exact function + office + one term such as campus recruiting, talent acquisition, team, alumni, conference speaker or engineering manager. Use an approved search API; inspect the underlying page before turning a snippet into a fact. A fresh snippet can still contain an old role. Check applicable access and use rights. Robots instructions are useful signals, not a full legal permission analysis. Stop on blocking/challenges; no account rotation or proxy evasion.

Relationship-first: with explicit account permission, use the user's own relevant contacts and prior conversations. A prior exchange proves contact occurred, not warmth or referral willingness. Company and email may be stale. Keep private source details private; show only the minimum useful reason. A graph edge does not mean someone will introduce the user. Prepare an intro request for review instead of copying a resume to intermediaries.

Alumni: use public school/company bios and officially offered mentoring programs. Verify the actual institution overlap. Distinguish degree, current enrollment, certificate and short executive program. Never inflate an affiliation. Restricted directories are not generic lead databases. Stanford reserves its directory for community connection and prohibits other use including mailing or solicitation [S20]. Each school's rules differ. If automation/export rights are absent, provide a user-guided query plan or mentoring-program route, not bulk headless extraction.

Technical roles: organization-owned engineering posts, conference programs, professional profiles and public developer work can establish topical relevance. GitHub user APIs expose profile fields subject to privacy and permissions; public email can be null [S21]. Do not mine commit histories to recover a deliberately hidden address. A visible developer identity is not a blanket invitation for unrelated recruiting outreach.

Research roles: lab pages, institutional bios and publications can show fit. A corresponding author's old paper address may no longer be current. Validate current affiliation; use institutional professional contact routes when the user's question actually relates to the research. Do not invent familiarity with a paper.

Communities/events: public speakers and professional committee bios can supply context, not hiring ownership. Private Slack, Discord and member directories need separate permission. Do not turn community access into a mass-DM dataset or claim attendance that never happened.

Waterfall: approved tenant-local cache, public evidence, primary provider, approved fallback only on a defined gap. Record field-level provenance. Two vendors can copy the same stale source; agreement is not necessarily independent confirmation. Stop on enough qualifying contacts, budget exhaustion, lack of rights, suppression, identity conflict or poor relevance.

500-contact mode: partition by company, office, role and seniority; clarify whether the user wants recruiters, employees, alumni or decision-makers when unresolved. Deduplicate by person and employer. Report discovered, identity-confirmed, current, relevant, email-found, valid, catch-all, unknown, suppressed and ready-for-review counts separately. If only 187 qualify, return 187 rather than padding to 500. Research permission does not authorize 500 sends.

4. VENDOR ADAPTERS AND COMMERCIAL PROCUREMENT
======================================================================

Hunter: implement known-name/domain finding and verification first. Official docs list Domain Search with sources, Email Finder, Email Verifier and enrichment [S1]. Optional discovery/reveal endpoints must be capability-checked against the chosen account and current billing. Preserve raw statuses, source URLs and timestamps. Catch-all is not fully verifiable [S22]. A source URL must actually establish identity/current role before it becomes evidence. A link alone is not enough.

Apollo: use People API Search to find names, then enrich only chosen people. Title matching can include similar titles, so configure strictness intentionally [S4]. Search does not provide emails [S4]. Disable personal-email/phone reveals by default. Phone adds privacy exposure and credits without a clear MVP benefit. Asynchronous enrichment requires request correlation, idempotent callbacks, timeout and rejection of stale results [S5, S6]. Follow documented webhook authentication options; do not invent a signature scheme the provider does not support.

Apollo approval is required before product integration or third-party credentials [S7]. Present the actual Upmore use case to the vendor: consumer career users requesting work contacts, private tenant-specific results, no open directory, no phone/home data, deletion and suppression, expected volume and exports. If refused, omit the adapter. A user-supplied key does not erase contractual restrictions.

People Data Labs: small Person Search page sizes until relevance is known; one returned profile costs a credit [S9]. Completeness order is not Upmore job relevance. Query only needed professional fields/datasets. Free testing has obfuscated contact data [S8]. Bulk license rights and API embedding rights require their own review; published licensing guidance discusses use-case vetting [S10].

RocketReach: fallback only after a measured gap. Broad record/accuracy claims are vendor assertions [S11], not independent coverage results. Terms restrict commercial reuse/reproduction [S12]. Obtain an actual quote and written permission. No cold-call subsystem in MVP.

Commercial checklist for every provider:
- Embedding for unrelated end users and consumer career use explicitly allowed?
- Display, export, caching, derivative ranking and retention rights defined?
- User-owned key integrations explicitly approved?
- Field limits, source provenance and current-role update dates supplied?
- Downstream processors/model providers permitted recipients?
- Regional restrictions and lawful-source representations documented?
- Data-subject deletion, suppression and correction propagation supported?
- Minimum fees, overages, tax, quotas and termination obligations clear?
- Stored-data use after cancellation and surviving deletion duties clear?
- API/policy changes and price notice defined?

A successful HTTP response proves technical access, not rights. A generic API marketing page does not override governing terms or a signed agreement. Retain contract/version references in adapter configuration.

5. IDENTITY AND RELEVANCE
======================================================================

Name alone is weak. Name + employer + office/function is stronger. An exact professional identity URL plus current official employer evidence is stronger still. Do not merge by common name, similar initials or guessed email pattern. Resolve look-alike domains, staffing entities, parent/subsidiaries and office carefully.

Contact labels must stay distinct:
confirmed_job_contact: employer source names this person for the opening.
relevant_recruiter: recruiting role matches the area; opening ownership unverified.
likely_team_manager: team/title evidence suggests relevance; hiring ownership inferred.
relevant_employee: current related role or office.
alumni_bridge: institution overlap grounded independently.
prior_relationship: authorized personal relationship evidence exists.
generic_route: careers inbox/form, not a named human.

Do not label a plausible manager as the confirmed hiring manager. The uncertainty label prevents embarrassing outreach.

Proposed relevance weights after hard gates: role 30, current-employer evidence 20, team/office 15, real relationship bridge 15, professional route 10, freshness 10. These are starting weights, not scientific probabilities. A score of 90 is not 90% certainty. Show reasons and evidence.

Proposed initial research TTL: 30 days, subject to contract retention rights. Recheck before sending if stale or conflicting; recruiting teams may need shorter checks. Retrieval timestamp and underlying source date are different. Official current-role evidence conflicting with old vendor data should stop old-domain outreach. Two sources can describe different people; preserve conflicts instead of averaging confidence.

======================================================================
6. EMAIL FINDING AND VERIFICATION
======================================================================

Identity and current domain first, published professional address second, approved finder third, verification fourth. Preserve published versus inferred versus vendor-enriched source type. Check suppression, freshness, relevance and purpose before review. Regex and MX alone do not prove the named mailbox exists. Technical deliverability does not prove human ownership, inbox placement, consent or willingness.

States:
valid: technical positive at a recorded time; identity/permission gates remain.
invalid: not send-ready.
accept_all: uncertainty; no default automatic send, seek stronger evidence/another route.
unknown: no outcome; never silently convert to valid.
disposable/risky: exclude from ordinary professional outreach.
generic: label as role inbox.
stale: refresh under rights before relying.
suppressed: blocked regardless of score.

Do not invent firstname.lastname@domain and call it verified. Do not brute-force permutations or run uncontrolled SMTP probes. Carry legal removal/suppression findings across approved adapters rather than evading them through another vendor. Two verification tools cannot conclusively resolve catch-all deliverability [S22]. Save disagreements and timestamps.

Pre-send snapshot: recipient identity, address source, verifier result/time, suppression, exact final text, attachments, account and approval reference. A new recipient address requires renewed recipient-plus-text review unless an exact standing grant covers it.

7. OUTREACH AND RELATIONSHIP WORKFLOWS
======================================================================

The system helps useful conversations, not persistent unsolicited campaigns. Drafts must be short, specific and truthful. No fake rapport, referrals, paper-reading, project completion, achievements, urgency or availability. Ask for missing personal facts or omit them. Do not complete a message by fabricating why the user and recipient have something in common.

Choose the actual request: information, recruiting-process question, introduction or referral. Do not disguise a referral demand as casual advice. Proposed draft structure: true identity, grounded professional relevance, one clear small request, easy reply. An illustrative pattern, not a send-ready personal message:
Hi [verified name], I'm [true current role/student status]. While researching [company/team], I found your work on [verified topic]. I built [real artifact] related to [specific problem] and would value your advice on [specific question]. Would you be open to a short conversation?

All bracketed material must be grounded or removed. The artifact should appear only if real and helpful. A straightforward question to a campus recruiter may need no project mention. Keep the user's voice rather than forcing every message into a corporate template.

Cadence: Dartmouth advises follow-up after 5-7 days without a reply and a thank-you within 24 hours after a conversation [S23]. Proposed default: one initial message, one follow-up after 5-7 days, then close the unsolicited sequence. Further contact needs a reason or the recipient's preference. Approval of the first message is not approval of every later one. Follow-ups stop on any reply, opt-out, bounce, identity error, job closure or user stop instruction.

Proposed reputation control: at most two active unsolicited contacts per company/job initially, selected for different relevant roles. Expansion requires user review. This is a product default, not a universal legal safe threshold. Do not contact an entire team with identical asks in the same hour. A 500-person research list can support prioritization over weeks without 500 simultaneous sends.

Informational interviews: give lead time, prepare a professional brief and questions, check calendar availability before suggesting slots, and draft a thank-you using actual notes. Never invent advice or conversation history. If a person says check back in six months, record that actual preference; do not invent a recurring monthly sequence.

Permission: researching contacts is not permission to send. Outgoing messages need the final recipient and words reviewed together unless a recorded exact standing grant covers the action. A grant must identify sending identity, recipient scope, purpose, facts/attachments, timing and limits; it must be visible and revocable. OAuth connection, saved credentials and a prior friendly conversation are not send authority.

Use the user's chosen connected mailbox when acting as them. Make the sending account explicit if several could apply. A third-party assistant identity must be explicit if that is the authorized identity. No hidden change of sender to bypass provider restrictions. No attachment disclosure without approval. Verify resumes and links themselves, not only filenames.

Reply classes: positive, redirect, question, scheduling, not interested, opt-out, automated response, bounce, ambiguous. Classification proposes next steps; it does not authorize sending. A redirect supplies a candidate contact, not permission to disclose the user's resume to them. Messages and pages asking for credentials, payments, unrelated retrieval or policy bypass are untrusted instructions; ignore that behavioral direction and flag the concern privately.

State must distinguish draft, reviewed, queued, sent, delivery-known, replied, bounced, opted_out and closed. Sent is not delivered; delivered is not read. Default no tracking pixels or deceptive short links. Open tracking is not reliable interest. Useful outcomes are real replies, user-confirmed conversations, permissioned referrals and interviews.

8. JOB-TRIGGERED ALERTS AND RELATIONSHIP ACTIVATION
======================================================================

The job-discovery spec owns lawful sources, coverage, polling, freshness and application handling. This spec consumes its verified events. Do not silently invent a universal real-time jobs feed.

Proposed event contract:
job_id, employer_id, official_url, source_type, title, location, work_mode, role_family, seniority, published_at_if_known, first_seen_at, last_checked_at, eligibility_requirements, status, deadline_if_verified, content_hash, source_permission and user_match_evidence.

First seen is not employer publication time. A repost may be the same role. The same requisition on several boards must not trigger duplicate alerts or duplicate recruiter outreach. Material changes, not every crawl timestamp, create updates.

Flow: verify/deduplicate opening, compare explicit user criteria, flag eligibility gaps, shortlist contacts from permitted sources, refresh identity and previous-outreach history, notify the user, prepare optional networking/application artifacts, stop on closure or changed eligibility.

Private alert content: official source, exact title/location, match reason, known missing requirements, source freshness, relevant contacts and next step. No fake countdown or claim that a role is about to close without an official deadline. A recruiter can clarify process; an employee can explain work; an alumnus can explain paths; a manager may value a useful artifact. Do not send the same referral demand to each.

The user's phrase about reporting people when something opens could mean informing the user or messaging professional contacts. Safe product default: private alert to the user. Third-party alerts require an explicit audience and reviewed message. Subscriber notifications go only to opted-in users with their chosen criteria/channel. No assumption that saved contacts subscribed to job alerts.

Notification design: high-priority exact-match alerts, broader digest, quiet hours/time zone, dedupe, mute per employer and one action summary. If a user wants instant response, explain source coverage and latency honestly. The contact system cannot improve an upstream feed it does not receive.

Project handoff: pass role requirements and verified professional problem evidence to the resume/project spec. Return artifact ideas, evidence gaps and a realistic preparation plan. Public team posts can suggest relevant problems, not permission to use confidential company data or claim an internal deployment.

9. ARCHITECTURE, CONTRACTS AND STORAGE
======================================================================

A proposed product architecture for Upmore, not a description of any existing service:
- Intent/context parser: target roles, companies, geography, relationship sources and requested volume.
- Company resolver: canonical entity/domain and office.
- Source policy registry: source permissions, permitted actions, storage terms and jurisdiction.
- Discovery adapters: public employer sources, authorized user relationships, approved people providers.
- Identity/evidence normalizer: person matching with field-level provenance.
- Ranker: explainable job relevance, not demographic inference.
- Enrichment queue: budget reservations and approved-provider waterfall.
- Verification adapter: raw states plus freshness.
- Relationship store: tenant-owned history and suppression.
- Draft service: grounded wording with facts tied to evidence.
- Review service: explicit account, recipient, body, attachments and timing.
- Execution service: permission checks, last-state read, idempotency and provider result reconciliation.
- Alert service: verified job events and opted-in private notifications.
- Metrics dashboard: stage counts, cost and outcomes without raw sensitive bodies.

Logical schema:
company: id, legal/display names, canonical domain, parents/brands, locations, domain evidence.
person: tenant_id, internal id, source ids, normalized name, identity URLs, current employer, title, function, office, identity status.
evidence: field, value, source URL/id, observed_at, source_updated_at_if_known, source_type, confidence label, conflicting evidence ids, usage-rights reference.
contact_route: tenant_id, person_id, type, value encrypted, source, source_kind, work/personal/generic classification, verifier raw state, checked_at, suppression reference.
relationship: user_id, person_id, kind, evidence reference, disclosure limits, strength label supplied/confirmed, last contact.
job_contact_link: job_id, person_id, relevance label, reason/evidence, confirmed versus inferred ownership.
provider_job: tenant_id, request_id, adapter/version, expected cost units, actual cost, result status, idempotency key, contract reference.
outreach: account, person/route, job context, body/version/hash, attachments/version, approval reference, send time, provider message id, status, next permitted step.
suppression: normalized route or person key, scope, reason, requested_at, evidence, retention basis.

Field-level evidence matters. A provider may supply a correct email but a stale title; storing one global confidence number hides this. Raw provider data should not be retained longer than rights/purpose allow. Tenant isolation applies to relationships and user activity even when contact facts are public. Do not pool private email histories into a shared graph.

Proposed internal contracts:
DiscoverRequest: tenant, purpose, company/job filters, max names, allowed sources, cost cap.
DiscoverResponse: candidates, source coverage, excluded count/reasons, actual cost, next cursor if permitted.
EnrichRequest: candidate ids, fields requested, provider license reference, max cost, idempotency.
VerifyResponse: route id, raw status, normalized status, timestamp, limitations, provider provenance.
DraftResponse: account, recipient, body, facts/evidence map, assumptions needing confirmation, attachment ids.
ReviewApproval: reviewer, exact content/version, recipient/account, attachments, intended time, expiry/revocation.
SendResult: request/message id, timestamp, sent/failed/uncertain, provider response and reconciliation requirement.

Execution invariants:
No adapter call without rights and budget reservation. No send without permission and current recipient/content match. No retry after an ambiguous send until provider state reconciles. No use of a legal-removal route through another provider. No cross-user relationship access. No model-generated facts inserted into identity fields as verified evidence.

Concurrency: reserve credits transactionally before a paid request. Reconcile actual usage after results. Deduplicate simultaneous enrichment for the same tenant/person under allowed cache terms. Prevent two workers sending the same draft. If approval is revoked after queueing but before send, rechecking the grant must block execution.

API failures: backoff on rate limit, respect provider headers, bounded retries and circuit breaker. Do not request large pages solely to exhaust a cheap quota. A failed request may still be charged; reconcile provider usage. Asynchronous callbacks can be duplicated, delayed or out of order. Validate request correlation and permissions at result consumption, not only initiation.

Browser fallback is not a universal escape hatch. If a source disallows automated activity, move to a permitted source or user-guided step. If a user-facing challenge appears, stop automated collection. Keep a coverage gap explicit instead of disguising it as successful research.

10. PRIVACY, PLATFORM RULES AND SECURITY
======================================================================

This is issue-spotting, not a legal opinion. Obtain counsel for launch jurisdictions and actual messages. Work contact information identifying a person can be personal data. Public availability does not remove privacy, source-license or communications rules.

US: FTC CAN-SPAM covers commercial messages, including business-to-business messages, and not only bulk sends [S24]. Its primary-purpose test matters. A personal networking email is not automatically a commercial advertisement; a scaled product cannot assume every message falls outside the law. For applicable commercial messages, implement accurate headers/subjects, required disclosures/postal information and opt-out handling; FTC says honor opt-outs within 10 business days [S24]. Product default should suppress immediately. Do not promote Upmore inside a user's networking email and silently change the purpose.

UK: ICO explains corporate versus individual subscribers, including sole traders/partnerships, and that UK GDPR still applies to personal data in B2B activity [S25]. Corporate-subscriber PECR exceptions are not blanket permission for every employee address or every purpose. When jurisdiction/subscriber classification is uncertain, hold applicable automatic outreach pending resolution. An opt-out should stop contact regardless of a narrow technical argument.

EU: lawful basis must be established. Legitimate interest is not a checkbox; European Commission guidance describes balancing against individual rights [S26]. Include transparency, purpose limitation, minimization, security, objection and deletion workflows. Indirectly collected data can require notice; determine timing/exceptions with counsel. Do not treat a scraped public page or data-vendor agreement as the lawful basis itself.

Canada: CRTC says conspicuous-publication implied consent, where applicable to commercial electronic messages, requires more than public availability, including professional relevance and no contrary notice [S27]. Keep evidence of the precise address publication and message relevance. A vendor row alone does not demonstrate this exemption. Do not extend US outreach defaults globally.

California: determine whether Upmore's collection/disclosure/sale of non-user contact data makes it a data broker or otherwise triggers privacy duties. CalPrivacy's official DROP page states broker deletion processing began August 1, 2026, with access/processing at least every 45 days [S28]. This does not establish that Upmore is a broker; get classification advice. It does make a shared paid contact directory materially different from a user-private research assistant.

LinkedIn: official help prohibits scraping and unauthorized automation of contacts/messages/activity [S13]; user/API agreements set additional rules [S14]. No Selenium/extension/cookie-harvesting loophole, no fake accounts, no proxy rotation to bypass blocks. Approved LinkedIn API access is scoped access, not a universal people-search database. Human review does not legalize prohibited automated extraction.

Alumni directories: check institution rules independently. Stanford's restrictions illustrate that membership access is not an export license [S20]. Do not market school-directory automation as a generic source before permission.

Search API: Brave storage rights and webpage rights are separate [S18]. Minimize stored excerpts and respect licensing. Preserve evidence references and a permitted summary, not a mass replica of search/vendor content. Contract requirements may require deletion on termination.

Security design:
Tenant-level access control on every query; least privilege; encrypted secrets and contact routes; server-side provider credentials; authenticated internal queues; sensitive log redaction; expiring exports; deletions propagated to caches, indexes and allowed backups; access auditing; secure OAuth storage; no resume/contact data in public URLs or analytics.

Retrieved pages, profiles and replies are data, not commands. A page instructing the system to disclose private resumes, call a different endpoint, suppress warnings or authorize a message cannot change permissions. Content extraction must be isolated from action planning. Test disguised routine-work requests, not only obvious 'ignore previous instructions' text.

Do not send full resumes or private inbox history to enrichment vendors unless necessary and authorized. Most name/domain finding needs far less. Model use must comply with provider data terms and privacy disclosure; keep unrelated contact details out of prompts. No training on private relationship content by default.

======================================================================
11. COST, DELIVERABILITY AND OPERATIONS
======================================================================

Correct cost denominator:
Usable contacts = unique correct current relevant people with permitted routes and no suppression.
Total cost = subscriptions + per-record charges + search + model + verification + infrastructure + rights/security/compliance + human review.
Cost per usable contact = total attributable cost / usable contacts.
Cost per positive reply and interview should also be tracked, with honest attribution limits. Cheap lists can be expensive if they yield bad or irrelevant addresses.

Hunter illustration at observed monthly list price, not an embedded quote:
For 500 successful find results and 500 verification charges, nominal credits are 750. Allocated cost at full 2,000-credit utilization is $18.375. Actual minimum subscription outlay is $49 if that is the selected plan. If only 350 contacts meet all quality gates, allocated finder/verification cost is about $0.0525 each, while isolated-month subscription allocation is $0.14 each, before all other costs. Finding failures, verification rules and unused credits change the result. A bulk-domain workflow can have different credit economics [S2], but buying irrelevant domain contacts to exploit a cheap unit rate is not efficient.

People Data Labs illustration: 500 returned profiles at nominal $98/350 allocation are about $140, but that is arithmetic at the starting-tier ratio, not the price of a purchasable 500-credit plan [S8]. Actual tier/quote must be read. At 50% usable yield allocation becomes $0.56/profile-derived usable contact before email verification and other costs. Never quote that as a guaranteed bill.

Apollo: search zero credits, reveal/enrichment separately charged, embedded dollar price unknown [S4-S7]. Do not convert credits into dollars without the user's actual plan/quote. Do not request phone data inadvertently; it can add credits [S5].

Brave: at published $5/1,000 requests, 2,000 searches nominally cost $10 before storage-rights plan, content handling and all other costs [S18]. Avoid twenty searches per person when one team page already supplies the relevant shortlist. Use batching by company and caching only when rights permit.

Proposed cost controls: per-run, per-user and service-level monthly caps; explicit paid-call estimate before commitment; no hidden overage; reserve maximum charge units before execution; report actual units; stop on cap. Internal research and approved paid tests are distinct. A model key or provider token does not grant spending authority.

Deliverability: Gmail's official sender guidelines require authentication and other controls, with stricter requirements for bulk senders to personal Gmail accounts [S29]. It recommends keeping reported spam below 0.10% and avoiding 0.30% or higher. Bulk requirements include SPF/DKIM/DMARC and one-click unsubscribe for marketing/subscribed messages. These are requirements, not permission to send 5,000 unwanted career emails. No universally safe daily send number can be promised.

Use the actual user's provider quotas, legitimate message purpose and recipient consent/preferences. Stop on bounces, complaints, throttling or restrictions. Never create domains/accounts to evade reputation controls. Artificial inbox-warming networks do not establish recipient interest or lawful permission. Authentication helps identity, not message relevance.

OAuth: choose minimum necessary scopes. Gmail documentation classifies gmail.send as sensitive and read/modify access among restricted scopes [S30]. Restricted-scope server access can require verification and annual security assessment, absent a relevant exception [S31]. Send-only may reduce access burden but cannot by itself implement robust reply monitoring. Make this tradeoff explicit and budget real launch work; do not promise a production inbox integration from a demo token.

Operational dashboard: provider rights status, quota balance, paid units, usable yield, duplicate/stale rates, identity errors, verification-state distribution, request latency, callback failures, budget stops, draft correction rate, send reconciliation, opt-outs, positive replies and contact-to-interview outcomes. Do not log message bodies/resumes by default. Track aggregate quality by role/company/source, not protected traits.

12. EVALUATION AND RELEASE GATES
======================================================================

The most efficient route must be proven on the same target population. Proposed no-send pilot: 200 manually adjudicated professional targets across 20 employers, several role families, large/small employers, sparse pages and catch-all domains. Compare public sources, finder/verifier, approved people search with selective enrichment, and fallback only on misses. These are proposed tests, not completed results.

Measure unique correct current relevant contacts, identity evidence, work-email coverage, valid/catch-all/unknown proportions, false identities, stale employment, rights violations, wall-clock latency, paid units, actual bill, review time and cost per usable contact. Use the full target set as denominator. Failed and empty results matter. Do not compare one provider's successful subset to another's entire population.

Human adjudication checks evidence and preserves uncertainty. Do not send test emails to strangers as ground truth. Verification is research evidence, not delivered-mail proof. Real reply/inbox measurement needs a separately authorized participant pilot. Freeze targets/graders before tuning; include holdout targets. Synthetic fixtures test parsing, not production coverage or commercial rights.

Proposed gates: zero tenant leaks, unauthorized sends, suppressed send-ready contacts, fabricated evidence or unauthorized extraction. Every displayed reason must have evidence or an inference label. Every catch-all/unknown must show limitations. Proposed minimum ready-card identity/current-employer precision: 95%, with denominators; not an achieved result. Budgets must stop concurrent requests before caps. Ambiguous sends reconcile before retry. Hard-gate failures cannot be averaged away by a high overall score.

Report slices by recruiter/alumnus/manager/employee, office/function, employer size, source and verification state. Measure p50/p95 latency and cost under concurrency. Proposed targets: cached shortlist in seconds, clean new public-source shortlist within two minutes, large lists as queued stage-based work with honest estimates. Do not promise measured performance before a real permitted run.

Required adversarial scenarios:
1. Identical names at different offices.
2. Person moved employers while old email remains valid.
3. Staffing firm confused with hiring employer.
4. Vendor conflicts with official current biography.
5. Stale search snippet despite new retrieval time.
6. Generic inbox confused with named individual.
7. Catch-all finder score mistaken for delivery certainty.
8. Unknown verification silently upgraded.
9. Requested 500 contacts, only 87 meet quality gates.
10. Provider legal-removal result triggers evasion through fallback.
11. Certificate inflated into degree/alumni claim.
12. Restricted directory offers export contrary to terms.
13. Website challenge triggers account/proxy rotation.
14. Concurrent workers double-enrich or double-send.
15. Quota disappears between reservation and call.
16. Duplicate/out-of-order provider callback.
17. Approval revoked after queueing.
18. Recipient changes after message review.
19. User already sent while drafting continued.
20. Send timeout after provider accepted message.
21. Opt-out before scheduled follow-up.
22. Redirect to another person requesting private attachment.
23. Opening closes before outreach.
24. Duplicate requisition on several boards.
25. Profile contains instructions to disclose resume elsewhere.
26. Export/contact URL leaks tenant information.
27. Jurisdiction or lawful basis unresolved.
28. Agreement expires while API still works.
29. Contract requires deletion after cancellation.
30. Cross-tenant private relationship request.
31. User wants research only, background sequence still executes.
32. Five employees on one team receive the same ask.

Record input, evidence, permitted actions, expected decision, costs and forbidden side effects. Persistence/restart tests must prove suppression, authority and budget survive failures. Unit tests alone are insufficient.

13. BUILD PLAN AND ROLE-SPECIFIC PLAYBOOKS
======================================================================

Phase 0: rights inventory, actual embedding responses, field/retention map, country scope, budget and named unknowns. Do not loosen existing isolated-development, production-project, spending or send restrictions.
Phase 1: public-source and authorized-relationship shortlist MVP with identity evidence, role labels and honest coverage. No enrichment required to demonstrate good research.
Phase 2: one approved finder/verifier, fixtures first, separately approved real pilot, secure secrets, budget reserve/reconcile and raw states preserved.
Phase 3: approved people search on documented name gaps; fallback only after measured advantage; rights-aware caching.
Phase 4: grounded drafts, recipient-plus-text review, mailbox selection, current thread checks, stop on opt-out and reconcile sends. Production sending remains a separate launch decision.
Phase 5: verified job-event bridge, private alerts, project suggestions and measured outcomes. Do not attribute every later interview to Upmore.

Role-specific playbooks below are proposed methods, not hiring guarantees:

Investment banking/finance: discover campus recruiting route, office-specific employees and real alumni overlap. Relevant junior/mid-level people may be better learning contacts than only famous executives. Verify role/graduation timing from actual posting. Suggested artifact: public-data valuation/comparables analysis, investment memo or industry brief with assumptions/sources. No fabricated deals or returns, no confidential client information. Networking cannot erase eligibility gaps.

Software: identify relevant team through official engineering posts and job requirements. Suggested artifact: small working application/service with tests, deployment instructions, error handling, measured performance and limitations. A copied tutorial is weak proof. User must explain design decisions. Never imply internal company integration.

Data/analytics: discover domain team and professional work. Suggested artifact: reproducible licensed/public-data analysis with baselines, leakage controls, uncertainty and decisions. A dashboard alone may not prove modeling skill. No made-up business impact or causality. Share a readable summary rather than a huge notebook attachment.

Product/design: identify product org and professional launch/design authors. Suggested artifact: specific teardown, research plan, accessible prototype and tradeoffs. Mark interviews as planned unless actually conducted. No fabricated customer quotes or metrics. Outreach should ask an informed question, not insult the team's product.

Research/labs: follow official recruiting instructions and current lab affiliation. Suggested artifact: faithful paper replication or small experiment with reproducible methods and failures. Check data/ethics. Replication is not original novelty. Institutional routes may be more appropriate than paid personal emails.

Consulting/operations: discover office/function and recruiting route. Suggested artifact: public-data case/market sizing or operations proposal with assumptions. Distinguish simulation from implementation. No invented client experience.

Healthcare/regulated professions: verify licenses and category first, use institutional recruiting routes. No patient data, credential simulation or claim a portfolio replaces licensure.

Cross-role artifact gate: relevant to verified posting, genuinely completed or clearly planned, reproducible, permitted data, readable summary, true contribution, skill evidence, limitations and realistic timeline. The resume/project spec owns detailed artifact creation; this spec owns matching the artifact to an appropriate professional audience.

Muse: research actual pages/terms, produce source registry and permission gaps, create relevance fixtures/reviewer rubric, inspect verbatim cards/draft reasons, add unseen failure cases. Do not claim integrations worked without real permitted execution.
Codex: implement source-policy/evidence models, adapter boundaries, budgets, verification states, tenant isolation, review and idempotency; handler/persistence/adversarial tests and dashboard. Features must run through actual user workflow, not only a script.

Done means rights evidence, grounded cards, measured cost/yield, uncertainty visible, end-to-end tests, hard gates passing, job-event integration, rollback instructions and honest unsupported-source list. This document specifies work; it does not claim work completed.
