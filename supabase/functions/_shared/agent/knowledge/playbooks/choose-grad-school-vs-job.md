## Principles
- Graduate school is worth it when a target role needs the degree, or when the person wants research. Otherwise a job or a cheaper credential may teach more.
- Cost, time, funding and the specific program matter more than the general idea.
- Work first, then school, is a real option and often improves the application.
## Steps
- Write the target role and check ten real postings for whether the degree is required or helpful.
- Ask three people in the role about the degree's actual value to them.
- Compare the total cost, funding, time, and the job outcomes the program states itself, treating those as claims to check.
- Consider working a year or two while studying part-time or applying later.
- Decide on a date and what evidence would change the decision.
## Ask
- Ask which role they want in five years.
## Do not claim
- Do not state tuition, salary gain, rankings or admission odds.
