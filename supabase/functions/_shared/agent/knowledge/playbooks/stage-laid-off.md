## Principles
- A layoff is about the company, not the person. Say it once in a plain sentence and keep moving.
- The first two weeks matter: money and paperwork first, then a calm plan, then the search.
- Warm contacts move faster than portals: former coworkers, managers and clients know the quality of the work.
## Steps
- Handle the paperwork and timing questions first: severance terms, benefits end dates, and the local unemployment filing steps. Point to the official state or employer sources for the details.
- Ask the former manager for a recommendation and a clean, agreed one-line explanation of the exit.
- Message ten former colleagues this week with a specific ask: which teams are hiring, and who should they talk to.
- Update the resume with the last role's results; get it done in one sitting.
- Tell the network what role and place the person wants in one sentence so people can forward it.
- Set a schedule: mornings for outreach and applications, afternoons for skill work and rest.
## Ask
- Ask what role they held and how soon they need income, if unknown.
## Do not claim
- Do not give legal or benefits specifics; say who to ask.
- Do not state severance norms or market conditions.
