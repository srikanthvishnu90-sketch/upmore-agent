## Format facts (sourced)

1. **Meta PM loop (standard)**: resume screen → recruiter screen → initial PM screen (product sense + analytical) → full loop of **45-minute** rounds: Product Sense, Analytical Thinking (Execution), Leadership & Drive (behavioural). A newer Central Products variant adds Product Sense with AI and a Product Architecture round.
   Source: IGotAnOffer Meta PM guide [commercial] (fetched) — https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/product-manager/facebook-product-manager-interview
2. **Meta PM timeline**: ~4–8 weeks on average (guide-reported).
   Source: same guide above.
3. **Google / Amazon PM**: Google screens behaviourally for "Googleyness" and Amazon maps behavioural answers to its 16 Leadership Principles in STAR — the same official Amazon guidance used for SWE applies to PM.
   Sources: IGotAnOffer PM behavioural guide [commercial] — https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/product-manager-behavioral-interview-questions ; Amazon (official) — https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-leadership-principles-interview
4. **Amazon PM-T wrinkle**: industry PM-T loops are reported as 7–9 conversations including a **1–2 page writing assignment** completed before the onsite, and a final loop of five 60-minute rounds. Treat as team-variable.
   Source: Aced Amazon PM-T guide [commercial] — https://www.aced.io/guides/amazon-technical-product-manager-interview?ref=exponent-blog.ghost.io
5. **Exponent**: named in the task brief as a canonical PM prep source; in this pass, Exponent material was only encountered second-hand (references inside other guides), and **no Exponent page was fetched directly — Exponent-specific format claims are UNVERIFIED here** and no item below relies solely on Exponent.
6. **Lewis Lin / *Decode and Conquer* & *The Product Manager Interview***: referenced across PM prep culture (164-question book). No publisher page was fetched in this pass, so Lin-specific frameworks are **not** cited as sole source for any item below.

## A. Product sense (10 items)

Taxonomy cross-check: Aakash Gupta's summary (citing Meta's scorecard) splits case-style product sense into Product Improvement, Product Growth, Product Launch, and Product Design — matching IGotAnOffer's Meta lists (design / improvement / strategy).
Sources: Aakash Gupta — https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/master-the-product-sense-interview ; IGotAnOffer Meta PM [commercial] — https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/product-manager/facebook-product-manager-interview

1. **Design a product for a segment** — e.g. "Build a product for group travel", "Design an app for pet owners", "How would you build Uber for kids?" Source: IGotAnOffer Meta PM (fetched list) above.
2. **Design a fitness app / new feature for an existing platform product.** Source: same list above.
3. **Improve a named product** — "How would you improve Instagram Shopping / Facebook Groups / Facebook Campus?" Source: same list above.
4. **Favourite product — what is it, why, how would you improve it?** Source: Aakash taxonomy (favourite-product as the laissez-faire product-sense form) — https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/master-the-product-sense-interview
5. **Improve engagement / retention for a specific product.** Source: IGotAnOffer Meta PM improvement list above; Aakash Growth type above.
6. **Product strategy: should the firm build/enter X?** — e.g. "Should Meta add a product for museums and their visitors?", "How would you decide to create a podcasting app?" Source: IGotAnOffer Meta PM strategy list above.
7. **Monetisation strategy** — "How would you monetise WhatsApp / Messenger?" Source: same list above.
8. **Launch / build-next strategy** — "What would you build next as PM in Google's consumer hardware org?", "How would you double YouTube's user base?" Source: IGotAnOffer strategy list [commercial] — https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/product-manager/product-strategy-interview-questions
9. **Segment-first discipline as a scored behaviour** — interviewers score whether user segmentation anchors the answer; a weak segment makes the rest of the answer hard to rescue (ex-Meta PM lead, via IGotAnOffer). Source: IGotAnOffer Meta PM (fetched) above.
10. **Product-sense timing/structure inside the round** — Lenny's guide breaks a strong answer into timed blocks (problem identification ~8–10 min, solution development ~8–10 min) with explicit "waypointing" between sections. Source: Lenny's Newsletter — https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-definitive-guide-to-mastering

## B. Execution / metrics (8 items)

Source for items 11–16: IGotAnOffer product-metrics guide [commercial] (fetched) — https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/product-manager/product-metric-interview-questions — which splits metrics questions into *metric definition* and *metric change*, and reports Meta runs an "analytical thinking" round and Google an analytical round at onsite.

11. **Define success metrics for a product/feature** — e.g. "What metrics would you use to determine success for Facebook Feed / Sponsored Posts?" (GAME-style structure: Goals → Actions → Metrics → Evaluation, as named in that guide.)
12. **Diagnose a metric drop** — "Facebook Groups usage is down 15% — how do you fix it?" / "Events 'I'm going' dropped 30% overnight — what data do you look at?" 
13. **Diagnose a funnel drop** — "Checkout conversion dropped 20% — what do you do?" (Amazon-reported form in the same guide.)
14. **Diagnose a surprising spike** — "YouTube DAU in Indonesia jumped 10% vs yesterday — what happened?" (Google-reported form in the same guide.)
15. **Set goals / measure a launch** — "How do you measure success after launching a product?" Source: IGotAnOffer PM behavioural guide [commercial] — https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/product-manager-behavioral-interview-questions
16. **Prioritisation / trade-offs under constraint** — what to build first, what to cut, and how you defend it with reach/impact/effort reasoning. Source: PM guide synthesis (RICE described) — https://publicreg.vaccination.gov.ng/Resources/tedir6/272014/ProductManagerInterviewQuestionsAnswers.pdf — **weak source (mirrored PDF); treat RICE-by-name as UNVERIFIED as a firm requirement — the underlying prioritisation *type* is attested by items 9–10 and the Meta analytical round description.**
17. **Analytical thinking = goals + analysis + prioritisation in one round (Meta's definition of the Execution round).** Source: IGotAnOffer Meta PM (fetched) — https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/product-manager/facebook-product-manager-interview
18. **Root-cause discipline (DEC-style structure as named by IGotAnOffer)**: Define the change precisely (definition, period, segment) → Explore internal vs external causes (MECE) → Conclude. Source: IGotAnOffer metrics guide (fetched) above.

## Worked example 1 — Product sense skeleton with metric tree (original)

Prompt: "How would you improve Facebook Groups?" (item 3)
1. **Clarify & goal (2 min):** Improve *meaningful* engagement for existing members, not raw group joins. Confirm scope: consumer Groups, mobile, worldwide.
2. **Segment (lynchpin, per item 9):** (a) admins of active groups, (b) lurker members (read, never post), (c) new joiners in week 1. Pick **lurkers in groups they joined >30 days ago** — largest pool, clearest unmet need.
3. **Pain points:** feed buries group posts under friend content; no low-effort way to contribute; notifications are all-or-nothing so they mute and churn.
4. **Solutions (generate 3, prioritise 1):** (i) lightweight reactions/prompts ("ask the group" templates) — low effort, high frequency; (ii) digest mode replacing per-post notifications; (iii) admin toolkit for welcome posts. Prioritise (i): highest reach × lowest build effort.
5. **Metric tree:**
   - North star: weekly active contributors per active group member.
   - Inputs: % lurkers shown a prompt → % who act → posts/comments per actor → replies per post (quality loop).
   - Guardrails: hide/mute rate, report rate, time-spent regret surveys, admin workload.
6. **Rollout:** A/B test on 5% of eligible groups, 2 full weeks, ship only if north star rises with no guardrail breach.

## Worked example 2 — Metrics-drop answer skeleton (original, using items 12/18)

Prompt: "Feed engagement is down 10% since yesterday."
1. **Define:** Which metric exactly (sessions? meaningful interactions?), which period, which segments (platform, country, new vs returning)? Get the interviewer to pin it — here: sessions, all users, since yesterday.
2. **Explore (MECE internal vs external):** Internal — logging/instrumentation change? release or outage? ranking change shipped? External — competitor event, referrer outage, holiday/weather, press cycle.
3. **Test in order of cheapness:** instrumentation first (is the drop real?), then segment cuts (one platform/country = product bug; uniform = external or logging), then timeline alignment with releases/outages.
4. **Conclude for leadership:** cause, evidence that rivals were ruled out, duration alignment, user impact, fix + monitoring — in that order.

## Worked example 3 — Estimation skeleton (original, item 20 type)

Prompt: "How many golf balls fit in a school bus?"
State assumptions, decompose by volume, correct for packing — order of magnitude is the score, not the digit.
- Bus interior ≈ 12 m long × 2.5 m wide × 2 m high ≈ 60 m³ gross; usable ≈ ⅔ after seats/structure ≈ 40 m³ (say so, don't hide it).
- Golf ball ≈ 4.3 cm diameter → treat as a ~4.3 cm cube for a first pass ≈ 80 cm³, then apply sphere-packing efficiency (~0.64, per the Fermi source above) in the other direction — either method lands in the **hundreds of thousands**.
- Answer as a range ("roughly 300k–600k, driven mostly by the usable-volume assumption"), name the weakest assumption, and stop. A defensible range + named assumptions beats false precision.

## Standardisation note
PM is the **least standardised** of the three families. The *round names* are stable at Meta (Product Sense / Analytical Thinking / Leadership & Drive) and the *question types* recur everywhere, but there is no shared problem bank like LeetCode/DataLemur, prompts are deliberately open-ended and firm-specific, and the best sources are commercial coaching guides reporting Glassdoor questions — not official firm publications. Teach types + structure, never "the questions".
