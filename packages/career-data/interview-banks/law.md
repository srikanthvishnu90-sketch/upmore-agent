# Law — Interview Question Bank (Big Law / summer associate focus)

*Product data. Researched 2026-10-13 from sources actually fetched (browser search + open). Items are paraphrased prompt types with short generic phrasings, not verbatim reproductions of any guide. Count: **32 canonical items** (6 personal/why-law + 6 why-firm/practice + 7 résumé/transcript/writing + 5 legal reasoning + 5 behavioural + 3 candidate questions counted as asks of the format — see §F note).*

*Scope caveat: the richest official question list fetched — Harvard Law School OPIA's Interview Questions page — is written for public-interest employers, but its personal, résumé, knowledge-of-organisation, legal-reasoning and behavioural categories are the same categories National Jurist's law-student guide lists for firm interviews. Where an item rests only on the Harvard page, it is paired with National Jurist for the firm context.*

## Format facts (sourced)

- **OCI / EIP screening interview:** short — about 20 minutes in National Jurist's OCI tips ("in just 20 minutes, interviewers will evaluate you"); LawCrossing's interviewing-tips guide puts screening interviews at typically 25 minutes, sometimes up to an hour, and on-campus interviews generally at 20–30 minutes with one lawyer. Sources: National Jurist, Top tips for on campus interviews — http://nationaljurist.com/top-tips-for-on-campus-interviews/ ; LawCrossing, Interviewing Tips for Law Students (search-verified PDF) — https://www.lawcrossing.com/article/pdf/900051010.pdf
- **Callback interview:** half a day at the firm, meeting individually with four or more attorneys for roughly 20–30 minutes each, often including a meal. Source: LawCrossing interviewing-tips guide — https://www.lawcrossing.com/article/pdf/900051010.pdf ; corroborated by LiveAbout's callback guide (series of attorneys plus at least one meal, firm customarily covers travel for out-of-town callbacks) — https://www.liveabout.com/how-to-get-a-biglaw-job-call-back-interviews-2164670
- **What must be answerable before any interview (Harvard OCS/OPIA 1L guide):** why law school, why this type of work, why this organisation — plus anything on your résumé and your writing sample; have 2–3 questions prepared per interviewer. Source: Harvard Law School, 1L Guide to Summer Jobs — https://hls.harvard.edu/bernard-koteen-office-of-public-interest-advising/opia-job-search-toolkit/1l-guide-to-summer-jobs/
- **Materials scrutiny is part of the format:** Harvard's guide warns candidates to be able to talk coherently about everything in the application package, including the writing sample; National Jurist likewise warns interviewers may go line-by-line through the résumé. Sources: Harvard 1L Guide (above); National Jurist, The interview questions law students should be ready to answer — https://nationaljurist.com/the-interview-questions-law-students-should-be-ready-to-answer/
- **NALP timing rules / offer windows:** NALP publishes recruiting principles and timing guidance for law-firm recruiting (historically governing when callbacks/offers happen); no specific current offer-window number was verified from a fetched NALP page in this pass — **UNVERIFIED for 2026 numbers; check NALP directly.** Background source fetched: NALP bulletin on early interview week — https://nalp-main-prod-app.azurewebsites.net/uploads/_bulletin_article_uploads/413_0606earlyinterviewing.pdf

## A. Personal / why-law (6)

1. **Introduce yourself** — a short narrative that lands on why you are applying for *this* position. Sources: Harvard Law OPIA, Interview Questions — https://hls.harvard.edu/bernard-koteen-office-of-public-interest-advising/opia-job-search-toolkit/interview-questions/ ; National Jurist — https://nationaljurist.com/the-interview-questions-law-students-should-be-ready-to-answer/
2. **Why did you go to law school / why did you choose law?** Sources: Harvard OPIA (above); National Jurist (above)
3. **What are your strengths and weaknesses?** (Harvard's list adds: a legal skill you struggle with.) Sources: Harvard OPIA; National Jurist
4. **Where do you see yourself in five / ten years? / short- and long-term goals.** Sources: Harvard OPIA; National Jurist
5. **Why this geographic area / this office's city?** Sources: Harvard OPIA (draw to a geographic area); National Jurist OCI tips (have an answer for why this city) — http://nationaljurist.com/top-tips-for-on-campus-interviews/
6. **Outside interests / something not on your résumé / latest non-legal book read.** Sources: Harvard OPIA; National Jurist (interests-section warning in the questions guide) — https://nationaljurist.com/the-interview-questions-law-students-should-be-ready-to-answer/

## B. Why-firm / why-organisation / practice area (6)

7. **Why do you want to work at our firm/office — as opposed to other firms doing similar work?** Sources: Harvard OPIA; National Jurist
8. **Which of our practice areas interest you, and why that specialisation?** Sources: Harvard OPIA; National Jurist (what area of law interests you; what interests you about our practice areas)
9. **What practice setting do you want (firm vs. government vs. legal aid/non-profit) and why?** Source: Harvard OPIA — *mainly public-interest framed; for Big Law this surfaces as litigation-vs-transactional and firm-size questions. UNVERIFIED as a verbatim Big Law prompt; the underlying "why this setting" ask is attested.*
10. **Why should we select you over the other candidates / what makes you a strong candidate?** Sources: Harvard OPIA; National Jurist
11. **What are you looking for in a job — your criteria, training/supervision expectations, ideal job?** Source: Harvard OPIA (knowledge-of-organisation category) — single-source, marked as such.
12. **Have you spoken with anyone who works here / what do you know about us?** Source: National Jurist — https://nationaljurist.com/the-interview-questions-law-students-should-be-ready-to-answer/ ; corroborated by National Jurist's firm-prep guide in search results — http://nationaljurist.com/how-to-prepare-for-your-law-firm-interview/?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=how-to-prepare-for-your-law-firm-interview

## C. Résumé, transcript and writing-sample scrutiny (7)

13. **Walk me through prior jobs** — responsibilities, why you chose those organisations, what you liked/disliked, why you left. Source: Harvard OPIA (résumé/transcript category)
14. **Why your undergraduate major / what did you do between college and law school / why the switch to law?** Source: Harvard OPIA
15. **Explain a grade** — Harvard's list includes being asked about a low grade ("LP") in a specific course; in Big Law terms: be ready to explain the weakest line on your transcript without being defensive. Sources: Harvard OPIA; Harvard OPIA's general warning to address transcript gaps/grades openly (same page)
16. **Tell me about your writing sample / legal note / a memo you wrote** — topic, argument, feedback you received. Sources: Harvard OPIA (legal-reasoning category); Harvard 1L Guide ("be prepared to answer questions about your writing sample") — https://hls.harvard.edu/bernard-koteen-office-of-public-interest-advising/opia-job-search-toolkit/1l-guide-to-summer-jobs/
17. **Tell me about your journal / moot court / clinic / externship / research project** — including the issue you argued and the other side's argument. Sources: Harvard OPIA; National Jurist (law review / moot court / clinic experience)
18. **What was the most interesting project you worked on / accomplishment you are proudest of (from your résumé)?** Source: National Jurist
19. **How has prior experience prepared you to practise law?** Source: National Jurist

## D. Legal reasoning / thinking (5)

20. **Favourite (or least favourite / most challenging) class — and why; favourite professor — and why.** Sources: Harvard OPIA; National Jurist (favourite/least favourite class; a case you read that you found interesting)
21. **Tell me about a complex legal issue you worked on / your hardest exam question.** Source: Harvard OPIA — single-source, marked as such.
22. **A recent Supreme Court case you disagreed with — and why.** Source: Harvard OPIA — single-source, marked as such. (In firm screeners this more often appears as "a case/deal you found interesting" — National Jurist.)
23. **What qualities make a good lawyer?** Source: Harvard OPIA
24. **A short hypothetical / "if you were the court, how would you rule?"** — reasoning and articulation are scored more than the "right" answer. Source: Harvard OPIA (anticipating hypothetical questions). *Note: Harvard attests hypotheticals chiefly for prosecutors/public defenders; sustained substantive hypotheticals are **not** a standardised Big Law screener feature — do not present them as one.*

## E. Behavioural (STAR) (5)

Harvard OPIA explicitly teaches the STAR frame (situation/task, action, result) for these. Source for the frame and items 25–28: Harvard OPIA — https://hls.harvard.edu/bernard-koteen-office-of-public-interest-advising/opia-job-search-toolkit/interview-questions/

25. **A time you made a mistake / failed — what happened and what you learned.** Also: National Jurist behavioural list — https://nationaljurist.com/the-interview-questions-law-students-should-be-ready-to-answer/
26. **A time you had too much to do and had to prioritise / competing priorities.** Also: National Jurist
27. **A time you worked with a difficult client, coworker or team member — or with people who did things very differently.** Also: National Jurist
28. **A time you persuaded someone / showed leadership / went above and beyond.** Also: National Jurist (leadership example)
29. **How do you work under pressure / are you a team player or do you prefer working alone / self-starter vs. guidance?** Source: Harvard OPIA (personal category)

## F. Questions the candidate is expected to ask (3 format asks)

These are not interviewer questions, but both guides treat "what questions do you have for us?" as a guaranteed, assessed moment — counted here as format items, not interviewer items.

30. **Ask what a successful summer associate does well here / about mentoring and professional development.** Source: National Jurist — https://nationaljurist.com/the-interview-questions-law-students-should-be-ready-to-answer/
31. **Ask the interviewer how they chose their practice area / what they enjoy most about working here.** Source: National Jurist (same page)
32. **Prepare enough tailored questions to fill the closing minutes (National Jurist OCI tips suggest 5–10 minutes' worth) and never ask something the firm's website answers.** Sources: National Jurist OCI tips — http://nationaljurist.com/top-tips-for-on-campus-interviews/ ; Harvard 1L Guide (2–3 questions per interviewer; don't ask anything Googleable) — https://hls.harvard.edu/bernard-koteen-office-of-public-interest-advising/opia-job-search-toolkit/1l-guide-to-summer-jobs/

## Standardisation note

Law is the **least standardised** of the three families: there is no shared question bank, rubric or timed station system across firms. What is standard is the *pipeline* (short screener → half-day callback), the *scrutiny objects* (résumé, transcript, writing sample) and a small recurring core (introduce yourself; why law; why this firm; which practice area; strengths/weaknesses; your questions for us) attested independently by Harvard's career guides and National Jurist.
