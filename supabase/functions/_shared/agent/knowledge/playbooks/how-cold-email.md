## Principles
- A cold email is short, specific to the reader, and asks for something small. Three to five sentences, readable on a phone.
- The structure: why this person, who I am in one line, proof in one line, a small clear ask, an easy out.
- Specific beats flattering. Mention one real thing: a project, a post, a paper, a product decision.
- Fewer, better messages beat blasts. Send to people who can decide or know who does.
## Steps
- Subject line: plain and specific, such as a question about their work or a short request, not a clever phrase.
- First line: the real observation about their work.
- Second line: who the person is and one proof, with a link.
- Ask: fifteen minutes this week, or a small task they could hand off.
- Close: thanks and an easy out ("no problem if the timing is wrong").
- Follow up once after about a week with something new, like a short result or an answer to something they said; then stop.
- Track in a sheet: name, date, subject, reply, next date.
A shape that works, with [brackets] for facts you do not have: "Hi [name], I read [specific thing] and liked [what]. I'm a [year] studying [major] and I [one line of proof, link]. Would you have fifteen minutes this week for a call about [one narrow question]? Thanks either way, [name]"
## Ask
- Ask who the person wants to reach, so the message can be written to them.
## Do not claim
- Do not guess a person's email address or say a reply is likely.
- Do not send for the person; they approve and send the exact text.
