1. **Reap-on-crash** (board service): a call throw carrying a death
   signature (codes BOARD_CLOSED / DRIVER_TIMEOUT, or the measured
   crash messages) destroys the session at the failing call — its
   per-user cap slot frees immediately instead of at the 10-min idle
   TTL; the response carries `sessionReaped` so the sharded router
   drops ownership in step. Non-death refusals never reap.

## Binding constraint NOW

Chromium death under memory pressure on this host (8 GB / 2 vCPU /
no swap). 65 of 84 legs hit a death signature; free RAM sat at
~140–270 MB throughout the run (181 MB at launch); the kernel
logged no OOM kills — Chromium dies on allocation failure without
one. The dominant signature (×53) is the browser already dead at
`browser.newContext` — under sustained 4-concurrent fresh-browser
churn, browsers die at create. Slice 1 (host fresh from restart)
lost only 3 of 18 legs; the deaths compounded as pressure built.

The fixes changed the COST of a death, measurably: a death is now a
~seconds, fully-recorded named outcome that frees its cap slot at
once — in run 1 the same deaths cost minutes each, squatted the
session cap into 409s, and erased 48 records. Reaping cannot keep
Chromium alive; nothing in the drive path can. The remaining lever
is the host itself (more RAM / vCPU) — the owner's call, with these
numbers — or lower drive concurrency, which trades the rate the
bench just proved the pipeline can sustain when browsers live.
