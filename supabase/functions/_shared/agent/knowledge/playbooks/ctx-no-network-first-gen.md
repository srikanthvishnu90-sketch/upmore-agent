## Principles
- A network is built, not inherited. The first contact is the hardest, and the quickest path runs through institutions that exist to help: the school, clubs, alumni, and employer events for early talent.
- People respond to short, specific, respectful messages from students; most are glad to help once.
- Do not hide being first in the family to do this; many people want to help for exactly that reason.
## Steps
- List every institution the person belongs to: school, department, clubs, hometown, faith or community groups, past jobs. Each has people to ask.
- Find ten alumni or members in the target field through the school's alumni tools and write each one a short message (see how-cold-email).
- Ask one question per message that takes a minute to answer, not a favor that takes an hour.
- Attend one career event or talk a week, learn one name there, and follow up the next day.
- Say thank you after each reply and report what happened; people help the ones who close the loop.
- Look for mentorship programs run by the school or community groups for first-generation students; treat each as unverified until read.
## Ask
- Ask what field they are aiming at.
## Do not claim
- Do not state that a specific program exists.
