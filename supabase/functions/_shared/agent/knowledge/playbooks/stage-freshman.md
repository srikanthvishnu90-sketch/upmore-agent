## Principles
- A first-year student with no experience is not behind. Most employers that hire first-years are smaller: small firms, startups, labs, nonprofits, local businesses, and a few early-exposure programs run by larger employers for first-years.
- The goal of the first year is proof and people, not a famous name: one real project, one real contact in the field, and a resume that shows initiative.
- Large-employer internship pipelines mostly favor students a year or two ahead, so aim there later and build toward it now.
- Paid is better, but unpaid or stipend work at a small place can still be worth it if it gives real tasks and a named person who will vouch for you. Check the school's rules on credit and pay.
## Steps
- Make a target list of 15 to 25 small organizations in the person's field and place (or remote): search the school career portal, the job board the school uses, local business listings and "startup" lists for their city. Check each careers page and the founder or team page.
- Build one small, visible thing in four to six weeks that fits the major: an analysis of a public data set, a tool, a short research summary, a design, a small site for a local business. Put it on one page with a link.
- Write a short cold email to a founder, manager or professor (see how-cold-email): who they are, one sentence on what the person built, one specific ask such as a short call or a small task. Send five to eight a week to people who can actually decide, not to a general inbox.
- Ask the school: career center, alumni office, department advisor, and professors who run labs. First-year help-wanted notes often live there and never reach job boards.
- Look for first-year and early-exposure programs by searching the program type plus the field, and read each page for the eligibility line before spending effort. Treat any program as unverified until the person has read its own page.
- Set a rhythm: one hour a day, a tracker with name, date sent, follow-up date.
## Ask
- Ask for the one thing that changes the plan most: the major or field they lean toward, or the city or remote preference if the field is known.
## Do not claim
- Do not name specific programs, firms, deadlines, stipends or acceptance odds.
- Do not promise an internship is available; say these are the places first-years actually find them.
