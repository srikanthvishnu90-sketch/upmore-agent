## Format facts (sourced)

- McKinsey, client-facing roles: a Personal Experience Interview followed by a Problem-Solving (case) Interview, sometimes plus an expertise interview; preceded in most consulting roles by Solve, a gamified problem-solving assessment for which the firm says no preparation is needed. Source: McKinsey & Company, Interviewing — https://mobileservices-uat.mckinsey.com/careers/interviewing
- BCG: application, then skill / case / team steps are described on its consulting interview-process pages; its case-prep page defines the case as working a realistic business challenge step by step — structure, questions, data analysis, quick calculations, identifying the key factors. Sources: BCG Careers, Case Interview Preparation — https://careers.bcg.com/global/en/case-interview-preparation ; BCG Careers, Consulting Interview Process (search-verified) — https://careers.bcg.com/global/en/interview-process/blogarticle/how-to-prepare-for-a-case-interview-a-bcger-s-advice
- Bain: case interviews are for consulting roles only; other roles use behavioural, portfolio, coding, written or online-assessment formats instead. In the live case there is not necessarily a single right answer — structure and reasoning are assessed. Sources: Bain & Company, Preparing for the Case Interview — https://bain.com/fr/careers/hiring-process/case-interview/ ; Bain & Company, Interviewing (search-verified) — https://Www.bain.com/careers/interview-prep/case-interview/
- Timing: no MBB firm page fetched stated a universal case length. A Stanford consulting-prep deck in search results described a common split of roughly 10–20 min fit, 20–30 min case, 5–10 min candidate Q&A — treat exact minutes as **UNVERIFIED / varies by firm and office**, not a firm promise.

## A. Case-interview structure — the steps every case expects (6)

1. **Clarify the objective** — restate the problem, confirm what success means, ask about constraints before solving. Sources: Bain (tips list: clarify objective, structure thinking, think aloud) — https://bain.com/fr/careers/hiring-process/case-interview/ ; BCG Careers (structure approach, ask thoughtful questions) — https://careers.bcg.com/global/en/case-interview-preparation
2. **Build a case-specific issue tree / framework** — break the problem into mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive branches tailored to this case, not a memorised template. Sources: CaseBasix BCG guide (MECE structuring step) — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview ; BCG Careers — https://careers.bcg.com/global/en/case-interview-preparation
3. **State and prioritise a hypothesis** — say which branch most likely drives the answer and test that first. Source: CaseBasix BCG guide (hypothesis-driven approach, steps 4–5) — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview
4. **Analyse exhibits / data** — interpret charts and tables, extract the implication, do not just read numbers back. Sources: BCG Careers — https://careers.bcg.com/global/en/case-interview-preparation ; CaseBasix — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview
5. **Do the calculations out loud, cleanly** — quick calculations are an explicit part of the BCG description; Bain likewise tells candidates to share reasoning and calculations. Sources: BCG Careers — https://careers.bcg.com/global/en/case-interview-preparation ; Bain — https://bain.com/fr/careers/hiring-process/case-interview/
6. **Synthesise top-down** — lead with the recommendation, support it with 2–3 findings, name risks and next steps, and be ready to defend it under follow-up questions. Sources: CaseBasix (synthesis step) — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview ; Bain (emphasise key points, ask thoughtful questions at the end) — https://bain.com/fr/careers/hiring-process/case-interview/

## B. Canonical case types (7)

Each type is attested by at least two sources fetched for this bank.

7. **Profitability** — profits/revenue are falling; isolate revenue vs. cost drivers (price, volume, fixed vs. variable cost). Sources: CaseBasix BCG case-type list — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview ; Bain official FashionCo. revenue-decline case (below) — https://bain.com/fr/careers/hiring-process/case-interview/
8. **Market entry** — should the client enter a new market/product, and how (build, buy, partner)? Assess market size/growth, competition, barriers, fit. Sources: CaseBasix — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview ; Bain external-case list on its own prep page ("Market entry in food and beverage") — https://bain.com/fr/careers/hiring-process/case-interview/
9. **Mergers & acquisitions** — should A acquire/merge with B? Strategic fit, revenue/cost synergies, valuation/funding, integration and regulatory risk. Sources: CaseBasix — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview ; consulting case-type roundups in search results (M&A listed alongside profitability/market entry/pricing) — https://ww2.jacksonms.gov/virtual-library/ZwDeOp/4OK087/CaseStudiesForConsultingInterviews.pdf
10. **Market sizing / estimation** — estimate a market or demand figure with little data: clarify scope, segment, state assumptions, calculate, sanity-check. Sources: Bain prep page ("Market sizing in travel and tourism") — https://bain.com/fr/careers/hiring-process/case-interview/ ; CaseCoach Bain overview in search results (market sizing definition and steps) — https://casecoach.com/b/bain-interviews/
11. **Growth strategy** — find new revenue: new segments, geographies, products, partnerships; prioritise by impact/feasibility. Sources: CaseBasix — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview ; Bain prep page ("Growth in public sector") — https://bain.com/fr/careers/hiring-process/case-interview/
12. **Pricing** — set or change a price using cost floor, competitor prices, and customer willingness to pay (cost-, value-, or competition-based). Source: CaseBasix pricing section — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview ; second attestation: case-type roundup — https://ww2.jacksonms.gov/virtual-library/ZwDeOp/4OK087/CaseStudiesForConsultingInterviews.pdf
13. **Operations / turnaround** — cut cost, remove bottlenecks, or revive a declining business. Sources: CaseBasix operations/supply-chain section — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview ; case-type roundup (operations improvement / restructuring) — https://ww2.jacksonms.gov/virtual-library/ZwDeOp/4OK087/CaseStudiesForConsultingInterviews.pdf

## D. Behavioural / Personal Experience Interview (6)

McKinsey's official page: prepare **two detailed personal examples for each of four impact areas**, focusing on your specific actions, the challenge, and the change you drove. Source for the format itself: McKinsey — https://mobileservices-uat.mckinsey.com/careers/interviewing
*Note on naming: the four area labels have been renamed over time and secondary sources disagree (older: personal impact / entrepreneurial drive / inclusive leadership; newer summaries: leadership, connection, drive, growth). The **labels are therefore marked UNVERIFIED as to current exact wording**; the underlying story types below are attested by multiple secondary sources fetched.*

20. **Leadership story** — a time you led/mobilised a team through a challenge, probed in depth on your individual actions. Sources: Management Consulted, McKinsey case interview / PEI — https://managementconsulted.com/mckinsey-case-interview/ ; Indeed McKinsey prep summary (search-verified) — https://sg.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/mckinsey-interview
21. **Personal impact / persuasion story** — a time you convinced someone who disagreed with you, without formal authority. Sources: Management Consulted — https://managementconsulted.com/mckinsey-case-interview/ ; Indeed summary — https://sg.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/mckinsey-interview
22. **Entrepreneurial drive story** — a time you started something / took initiative and pushed through obstacles or a tight deadline. Sources: Management Consulted — https://managementconsulted.com/mckinsey-case-interview/ ; Indeed summary — https://sg.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/mckinsey-interview
23. **Courageous change / growth story** — a time you drove change against resistance, or adapted/learned from a setback. Sources: Management Consulted (courageous change) — https://managementconsulted.com/mckinsey-case-interview/ ; Indeed summary — https://sg.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/mckinsey-interview
24. **Deep follow-up probing on ONE story** — expect 15+ minutes of "why did you do that / what else did you consider / what would you do differently" on a single experience; surface-level rotation through many stories fails this format. Source: Management Consulted — https://managementconsulted.com/mckinsey-case-interview/
25. **Why this firm / why consulting + your questions for the interviewer** — Bain states it looks, in all cases, for people who can explain why they want to join Bain specifically; BCG's own prep advice stresses the closing Q&A as part of the assessment of fit. Sources: Bain Interviewing (search-verified) — https://Www.bain.com/careers/interview-prep/case-interview/ ; BCG Careers blog, How to Prepare for a Case Interview — https://careers.bcg.com/global/en/blogarticle/how-to-prepare-for-a-case-interview-a-bcger-s-advice

## E. Math drills (5) — skills, not memorised questions

26. **Profit equation drill** — profit = revenue − cost; revenue = price × volume; cost = fixed + (unit cost × volume); margin = profit ÷ revenue. Attested as the core of profitability cases: CaseBasix — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview
27. **Break-even drill** — units needed = fixed costs ÷ (price − unit variable cost). This is the calculation at the heart of Bain's official Coffee Shop Co. case: Bain — https://bain.com/fr/careers/hiring-process/case-interview/
28. **Market-sizing arithmetic** — population × penetration × frequency × price, top-down and bottom-up, with a sanity check. Sources: CaseCoach Bain overview — https://casecoach.com/b/bain-interviews/ ; Bain — https://bain.com/fr/careers/hiring-process/case-interview/
29. **Percentage / growth drill** — % change, share, and growth-rate calculations done mentally, without a calculator, under time pressure. Source: CaseBasix (mental-math expectation) — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview ; BCG Careers ("perform quick calculations") — https://careers.bcg.com/global/en/case-interview-preparation
30. **Exhibit-math drill** — read a chart, compute the one number that matters, and state its "so what" in one sentence. Sources: BCG Careers — https://careers.bcg.com/global/en/case-interview-preparation ; CaseBasix — https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bcg-case-interview

## Worked example case skeleton (ORIGINAL — built for this bank to demonstrate the canonical structure; not a real firm's case)

**Prompt (original):** A regional coffee chain's profit fell last year despite opening new stores. Diagnose why and recommend what to do.

- **Clarify:** Profit fell in absolute terms or margin? Same-store or chain-wide? Timeframe = last 12 months? Objective = restore prior margin within a year?
- **Issue tree (MECE):** Profit = Revenue − Cost.
  - Revenue = stores × transactions/store × spend/transaction. Branches: footfall, conversion, basket size, price/mix.
  - Cost = fixed (rent, salaried staff, depreciation on new stores) + variable (beans/milk, hourly labour, waste).
  - External check: new competitor entry, input-price inflation — kept separate so internal/external do not overlap.
- **Hypothesis:** New stores diluted the chain — fixed costs rose faster than revenue, and same-store transactions fell.
- **Analysis (illustrative invented numbers):** Revenue +4% but costs +11%. Same-store transactions −6%; spend/transaction +3% (price rise worked, volume did not). Rent + labour for 5 new stores = most of the cost jump; 2 of the 5 trade below break-even volume.
- **Synthesis:** Recommendation first: pause openings, close or re-site the 2 sub-break-even stores, and run a footfall fix (daypart offer) in same stores before any further price rise. Support: (1) problem is cost/volume, not price; (2) price already proved elastic headroom is limited. Risks: lease exit costs, staff impact. Next steps: store-by-store P&L in 2 weeks, lease review, pilot the footfall offer in 5 stores.

## Standardisation note

Consulting is **moderately standardised**: the *process architecture* (fit/PEI + case, structure→analysis→synthesis, no single right answer) is published by the firms themselves, but the *exact case content and PEI area labels change* and are never published in advance beyond sample cases.
