## Principles
- Work authorization decides which employers can hire the person and when. It changes the plan, so learn the exact status before advising.
- Student status usually carries limited paths to work during and after study. The rules are specific to the status and the school, and they change, so the official source is the school's international office.
- Many employers say they do not sponsor; some who say so still hire students on work permits they already allow. Read each posting's authorization line, and ask the recruiter when unclear.
## Steps
- Go to the school's international student office first and ask exactly what work is allowed now, what paperwork is needed before starting, and what is allowed after graduation.
- Build a target list in two groups: employers that have hired students on the person's status (check the employer's own statements, alumni, and the school's career center), and employers in the field known to sponsor.
- Put the work authorization status in one honest line when asked, and prepare the answer so it takes ten seconds.
- Use alumni from the same country or school who got through; ask how they approached it.
- Search with sponsorship-friendly filters and read each posting; do not rely on the filter alone.
- Keep a second route: roles in the home country or regions with easier paths, and graduate programs, as options to compare later.
## Ask
- Ask what status they hold and when it ends, because this changes the plan the most.
## Do not claim
- Do not give legal advice or state rules, caps, dates or fees from memory; point to the school office and an immigration attorney.
- Do not name employers as sponsors without a tool result.
