## Principles
- Small firms and startups often do not post openings. They hire when someone useful shows up at the right time, so the approach is to make being useful obvious and cheap to say yes to.
- The decision maker is reachable: a founder, owner, or team lead. A short, specific email to that person beats a form.
- Offer a small, concrete piece of work, not just a request. A first task of a week or two lowers the risk for both sides.
## Steps
- Find them: search the person's city and field plus words like studio, agency, lab, startup, clinic, firm; browse local business groups, chamber pages, incubator and demo-day lists, and the school's alumni businesses.
- Pick the ten that do work the person can describe in one line. Read the site and one recent post or project each.
- Email the decision maker (see how-cold-email) with one specific observation about their work, one line of proof from the person, and one small ask: a fifteen-minute call or a first small task.
- Offer a trial: a one- or two-week project with a clear output. Agree what the person will deliver.
- Follow up once after about a week with a new useful thing, not a nudge.
- Ask each person for one referral to another small employer, whether or not they hire.
## Ask
- Ask the person's field and place, so the list can be specific.
## Do not claim
- Do not say a particular firm is hiring or pays interns.
- Do not guess email addresses without a tool or the person's source.
