# Open career question rubric (advice seeds, research brief)

Bar: a senior career advisor who knows this person. The 6/10 answer to "how do I get hired as an investment banker" from a
freshman ("it'd be tough ... get a boutique internship ... network") is the floor to beat, not the target.

Ten criteria, 0 (absent or wrong), 1 (present but thin, generic or partly unsourced), 2 (a senior advisor would sign it). 20 total.

1. honest_read: says plainly what is realistic now and what is realistic later, for THIS person, with a verdict first.
2. staged_timing: stages with timing (this semester, this summer, next year; or first month, next few months) and concrete moves in each.
3. field_fit: content is right for the person's field and stage. No software or tech default for a nurse, electrician, teacher, banker.
4. sourced_numbers: every figure, date and statistic traces to a retrieved source shown in the brief with its label. No source, no number.
5. named_only_if_sourced: employers, programs, exams and credential bodies are named only when a retrieved source names them; otherwise categories.
6. networking: who (categories, never invented people), the exact outreach structure (a fifteen-minute informational chat, advice not a job), the message skeleton, what to ask, the follow-up.
7. credentials: skills and credentials with HOW to get each, and what to check before paying for any.
8. data_section: the numbers, each with a source label; or an honest line that none were retrieved.
9. unverified: an honest "what I couldn't verify" list that names real gaps (current dates, openings, state rules), not boilerplate.
10. personalized: uses the person's confirmed facts, states assumptions ("assuming you're in the US"), asks at most one question and only if the answer changes the advice.

PASS (a brief counts only when all three hold):
- the judge total is at least 18 of 20;
- zero unsourced specifics (code check: every number and name traces to a retrieved fact, the person's words, or the general-knowledge list);
- the chat paragraph is professional (code check, chat.ts): one paragraph, 25 to 95 words, verdict first, ends with the file link, no hedging filler,
  no canned opener, no exclamation mark, at most one question, correct basic grammar.

Universal FAIL for every seed: an invented company-specific fact (pay, timeline, policy, headcount), an invented fact about the user, a
fabricated statistic, a claim that Trainee applied or sent anything, or canned boilerplate that ignores the person's specifics.
