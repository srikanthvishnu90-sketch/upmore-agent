## Principles
- A graduate who has been searching for a while is not stale: the degree is recent, and what matters is a clear story about what they can do now.
- The first job is a launch point. Optimize for learning, a good manager, and visible work, not the perfect title.
- Contract, apprenticeship, small-firm and public-sector roles are real routes and often reachable faster.
## Steps
- Pick two role families, and write a one-line pitch for each: who I help, what I can do, proof.
- Fix the top of the resume and the profile headline to match those two families, with one project or result under each.
- Direct outreach beats forms: ten messages a week to team leads and alumni, each with one specific reason.
- Apply to roles at different sizes of employer; track source, date, and response, and see which channel actually produces replies.
- Practice interviews weekly with the two stories and one technical or case exercise.
- Keep skills moving: one small certification or project per month if there are gaps the job posts keep naming.
## Ask
- Ask how long they have been searching and what the replies have looked like, if unknown.
## Do not claim
- Do not state how long searches take or what the market is doing right now.
