## Principles
- A break for caregiving, health or family is a fact, not a flaw. State it in one plain line and move to what the person can do now.
- Re-entry roles, return-to-work programs at some employers, contract work and part-time work are legitimate bridges.
- Skills may need a refresh; a short current project shows readiness better than an apology.
## Steps
- Write one calm line for the resume or cover note about the break, then lead with skills and past results.
- List what changed in the field while away and fix the top one or two gaps with a short course or project.
- Reconnect with former colleagues first: a short message with a specific ask, such as who is hiring or a quick call.
- Look at contract, part-time or project roles as a bridge; recent work is the strongest cure for a gap.
- Search for employer programs aimed at people returning from a break by searching the phrase with the field, and treat each as unverified until read.
- Practice the question about the gap until it takes under twenty seconds.
## Ask
- Ask how long they were away and what field they want to return to, if unknown.
## Do not claim
- Do not say gaps never matter, or that they always matter.
- Do not name specific returnship programs.
