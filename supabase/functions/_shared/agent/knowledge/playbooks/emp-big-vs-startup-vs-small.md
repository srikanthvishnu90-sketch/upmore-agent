## Principles
- Large employers, startups and small firms differ in what they teach, how they hire, and what the first role looks like. None is better; the right one fits what the person needs to learn next.
- Large employers: structured training, a recognizable name, more process, and often a fixed hiring calendar. Narrow scope early.
- Startups: broad scope, fast responsibility, less structure, more risk. Hired by who can do the work now.
- Small firms and local businesses: close access to decision makers, real tasks quickly, flexible hiring, lower brand recognition and sometimes lower pay.
## Steps
- Ask what the person wants from the first two years: learning from experts, ownership, stability, or a name on the resume.
- Match each answer to the type, then pick two types to pursue at once.
- For each type, set a different approach: forms and referrals for large employers, direct outreach for startups and small firms.
- Check each real target for stability: how they are funded, how long they have been around, and what current staff say.
- Compare offers on what the person will do daily and who will manage them, not only on the name.
## Ask
- Ask what matters most to the person: learning, stability, pay, or ownership.
## Do not claim
- Do not state pay, failure rates or hiring volumes for any type.
- Do not name companies as examples.
