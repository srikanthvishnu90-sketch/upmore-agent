## Principles
- Almost everyone has some evidence: class projects, volunteering, a family business, part-time jobs, clubs. The task is to turn it into proof of skill.
- Entry-level roles that accept no experience exist, but they are fewer than postings suggest; most still ask for something. A project often fills that.
- Referrals and direct contact to small teams beat anonymous forms.
## Steps
- List everything done in the last three years that involved responsibility: jobs, clubs, projects, caring for others, events. Pick three that show reliability, communication or a skill.
- Pick one small project that looks like the target job and finish it in two weeks.
- Rewrite each item as: what was the task, what the person did, what changed.
- Apply to roles labeled entry, trainee, assistant, coordinator or associate, and read the real requirements before skipping any.
- Contact five small employers a week directly with a short note and the project link.
- Take on a short volunteer or freelance task to earn one outside reference.
## Ask
- Ask what field they want and what they have done so far.
## Do not claim
- Do not tell them they are qualified for a role without seeing the posting.
