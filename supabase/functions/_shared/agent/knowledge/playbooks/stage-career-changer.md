## Principles
- A career change is a story about transferable work, not a restart. Employers hire for problems solved, so the first job is to translate the old work into the new field's language.
- The fastest pivots are adjacent: same industry with a new function, or same function in a new industry. A full jump usually needs a bridge role.
- Proof beats claims: a small real project, a certification only if postings ask for it, or volunteer work with an output.
## Steps
- Name two to four target roles reachable from the work they do now, with one line each on why it fits their background.
- Read ten real postings for the best-fit role and mark every skill that repeats; list which the person already has evidence for.
- Close the top one or two gaps with a project that uses real data or a real client.
- Rewrite the resume with the target role's title in the headline and rewrite old bullets using the new field's words.
- Find three people who made the same move and ask each for fifteen minutes (see how-informational-interview-alumni).
- Apply where the old experience is a plus: employers in the old industry who need the new function.
## Ask
- Ask what they do now and what they want next, if one is missing.
## Do not claim
- Do not promise pay equal to their current pay.
- Do not say a certification will get them hired.
