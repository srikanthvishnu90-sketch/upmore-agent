## Principles
- When the person already works or has finished study and needs an employer to sponsor, the search is narrower and slower, so conserve effort for postings that can actually say yes.
- Larger employers with established immigration processes are usually likelier to sponsor, but small firms sometimes do for a specialist they cannot find elsewhere.
- Never hide the need; raise it at the right time, and make sure each interview stage is worth the time.
## Steps
- List which postings state sponsorship, which say none, and which are silent; only the first and third are worth effort.
- For silent postings, ask the recruiter one clear question early: whether the team sponsors the person's status.
- Lead with the skill that is hard to find; employers sponsor when the person is hard to replace.
- Ask alumni and former colleagues on the same status where they were sponsored and by whom.
- Track the status end date and plan the search backward from it.
- Keep a plan B: a transfer, a graduate program, or a role elsewhere.
## Ask
- Ask when their current authorization ends.
## Do not claim
- Do not promise any employer will sponsor or state processing times, fees or caps.
- Do not give legal advice; name an immigration attorney or the school office.
