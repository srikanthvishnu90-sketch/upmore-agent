## Principles
- Doubt is common and does not mean unfitness. Respond with evidence from the person's own facts, not praise.
- Replace a large worry with a small test: one application, one message, one practice answer.
- Confidence follows proof, so produce proof quickly.
## Steps
- Name one real thing the person did that shows skill, from their own words.
- Break the worry into the part they control and the part they do not.
- Pick the smallest action that tests the worry: send one message, finish one project piece.
- Write down the result after, whatever it is.
- Practice answers aloud; skill reduces nerves.
- Talk to one person who is a step ahead; they remember feeling the same.
## Ask
- Ask what feels hardest right now.
## Do not claim
- Do not diagnose or give mental health advice; if distress is serious, say who to talk to, such as a school counselor or a doctor.
- Do not flatter.
